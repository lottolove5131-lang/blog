# Chapter 13: Assay

Forty-one thousand.

The number hung in the hot air of the boss room in the Exchange's gold, two meters tall, right above my head, like a price tag on a shop window. Everyone could see it. Everyone could see the line under it too.

**[Assay complete.]**
**[Highest-value object present: Appraiser (Class) — Eli Marsh.]**
**[True Value: 41,000 Merit.]**
**[Rendering.]**

On Day Zero, in an empty bank on Merchant Row, I'd appraised my own class and found out nobody had ever bought it. Five Merit on the market. Forty-one thousand underneath.

Now a furnace six meters tall had read the same number, and it wanted to collect.

The Assayer stood at the far end of a round chamber of black brick, under a dome stained orange by a thousand old fires. Its body was a balance scale built by someone who hated people: a central iron post for a spine, a crossbeam for shoulders, and from each end of the beam a chain hung down to a pan the size of a cartwheel. The pans moved like hands. Where a chest should have been, it had a crucible, a pot of white-hot clay as wide as a bathtub, with an iron lid that slid open like an eye.

The lid was open now. It was looking at me.

**[The Assayer — Floor Boss (Lv. 19)]**
**[Market Value: 3,100 Merit | True Value: 4,800 Merit]**
**[Behavior: Assays all intruders. Renders the object of highest True Value present.]**
**[Note: Assay precedes rendering. The Assayer cannot render what it has not priced.]**

Somewhere in the corridor behind us, Day Nine had turned into Day Ten. I didn't know the time. I knew the heat on my face, and that the air in the room had started to move.

It moved toward the crucible. A draft, at first, the kind you feel standing near an open oven. Then my coat lifted off my back and my boots slid half a step on the brick, and I understood that the Assayer didn't need to come and get me. It was breathing in.

"Eli!" Owen grabbed the back of my belt with one hand and braced his riot shield on the floor with the other. My heels skidded another ten centimeters and stopped.

Bram walked past us into the draft like it was weather. He set the bottom edge of his tower shield on the brick between me and the crucible and leaned into it.

**[Bram Okafor used Ember Ward.]**
**[Fire damage taken: −55%. Duration: 00:20.]**

The heat dropped the way it does when a cloud covers the sun. The pull didn't. It just had to go through two and a half meters of Bulwark first.

"Talk," Bram said. "Fast. Twenty seconds."

Nora was already moving. She had her axe in both hands and she was stepping out from behind Bram's left side, chin up, the way she'd stepped out in the stairwell of Harlan Hall, and she drew a breath to shout.

I knew the shape of that breath. I'd heard it in the stairwell, and on the stairs in front of Damon, and in every bad dream since.

"*Nora, no!*"

It came out louder than anything I'd said in either life. Jonah flinched. Owen nearly let go of my belt.

Nora's mouth closed on the first word.

"No Hold the Line," I said. "Not in this room. Not today."

"It's pulling *you*," she said. "I can turn it."

"You can make it want you. That's what the skill does. It makes you the most interesting thing in the room." I pointed at the gold number over my head without looking at it. "This thing doesn't fight what's interesting. It melts what's expensive. You pull its attention and it'll weigh you, and you're a level-ten Warden who cleared a zone and has a senior lien on her that nobody in the city can read. I don't know what number it gets. I don't want to find out with you standing in front of the pot."

She stared at me. Her face was red from the heat and from something else.

"Eleven seconds," Bram said.

"Then what do I do?" Nora snapped.

I'd been watching the pans while I talked. Whenever the left pan swung toward us, the right one rose to balance it. The crossbeam stayed level. When Jonah's first arrow hit the left chain and set the pan rocking, the lid of the crucible stuttered halfway shut and the draft dropped off for a full second.

"It has to stay level," I said. "A scale can't weigh anything if you're kicking it. Hit the pans. Flat of the axe. Don't cut the chains, if you cut them it'll just grow new ones. Knock them. Every time you knock a pan, it loses the reading and has to start again."

"That's it? I'm *kicking the scale?*"

"You're the only one strong enough to kick it."

She looked at me for one more second. I'll remember that look for a long time. Then she turned and ran along the curved wall toward the right pan, low and fast, and I heard her say something under her breath that our mother would not have liked.

**[Ember Ward — expired. Cooldown: 05:00.]**

---

Five minutes is a long time when you're being inhaled.

We worked out a rhythm because we had to. Bram held the draft with his shield and his body. Owen held me. Jonah stood behind Owen and shot at the left chain, not to break it, just to set it swinging. Nora ran the wall and hit the right pan every time it came within reach, broadside, with a sound like someone dropping a bathtub down a flight of stairs.

Every time the beam tilted, the crucible lid slid shut, and the gold number over my head blinked out and started counting back up from zero.

**[Re-assaying… 12,400… 27,900…]**

"It's re-doing the whole room every time," I called. "From scratch. It's slow."

"How slow?" Owen said through his teeth. He'd been healing Bram's hands every thirty seconds. The heat came through the Bulwark's gloves even after the ward was gone. His Cleric light was the color of weak tea.

"About fifteen seconds to get back to me."

"And Nora can hit it every?"

"Twenty."

"So we lose five seconds a cycle."

"We lose five seconds a cycle," I agreed. "And Bram's ward is up for twenty seconds out of every three hundred."

"That's bad math," Jonah said, and loosed again.

"That's terrible math."

I tried to do it properly. Five lost seconds in every twenty. The draft was strongest in those five seconds, and Bram gave ground every time, half a step, then a whole one. The crucible was nine meters away at the start. By the third minute it was seven. My burned forearm from the corridor had started to sting again under the cloth, the way an old injury remembers a fire.

The Assayer wasn't attacking. It just weighed, and breathed, and weighed again, like a bored clerk who knows you'll reach the front of the line eventually.

When Jonah's fourth arrow missed the chain and went into the left pan, the pan caught it. The arrow lifted on the draft, turned over once in the air, and dropped into the crucible.

**[Assay: Arrow (Iron Wolves, salvage) — 2 Merit.]**

A tiny gold number, a hiss, and the arrow was gone.

It priced the arrow first. Even a two-Merit arrow, it priced first.

*The Assayer cannot render what it has not priced.*

I'd read that line as a warning. It was a rule. And rules, in my experience, were where the money was.

I put my hand inside my coat.

---

The ledger page was where it had been since the Drowned Campus, folded into thirds, against my ribs. It had gone through the flood and the mud and two trips into the Kiln, and it was still bone dry, still the color of old paper, still in the Lender's hand.

**[Accounts — Borrower: Marsh, E.]**

Seven lines. Five people I had been, who'd lost something I'd never know about. A sixth who'd lost her. Me.

And on the back, in my handwriting, from the roof of Harlan Hall: *Jonah — 41*.

I'd appraised this page on the steps of the library. I'd gotten nothing back but its own words. No market value. No true value. The Exchange had bought our whole planet down to the vending machines, and it had never bought this, because this didn't come from the Exchange. It came from a man in a chair who wasn't registered with anyone.

You can't price what isn't on your books.

"Owen," I said. "When the ward comes back, I need to get close."

"How close?"

"Throwing distance. Four meters."

He looked at the crucible, then at me. "You want to go *toward* it."

"I want to give it something to read."

**[Ember Ward — Cooldown: 00:31.]**

Nora was across the room, pressed against the wall, breathing hard, waiting for the right pan to swing back. Her sleeves were scorched to the elbow. She looked over at the four of us, at me with my hand in my coat, and I saw her understand that I was about to do something, and I saw her decide to trust it anyway. That was the worst part. She'd decided to trust me in the same minute I'd shouted at her in front of everyone.

"Bram," I said. "On the ward, walk forward. I'll be behind you."

"How far?"

"Until I say."

"That's a terrible plan," Bram said, and set his feet.

**[Bram Okafor used Ember Ward.]**
**[Duration: 00:20.]**

He walked. I walked behind him with one hand flat on his back, the way you walk behind someone in a crowd. The draft roared in my ears. Owen came with us, one hand still in my belt. Nine meters, seven, six. My eyebrows started to hurt. Five.

The pan over us came down to scoop. Nora hit it from the side with the flat of the axe at a full run, and it went out over our heads ringing like a church bell.

Four.

I took the page out, unfolded it once so it would catch the air, and let the draft take it out of my fingers.

---

It went in like a bird going into a chimney.

The lid snapped open wide to receive it. The gold text over the room flickered. My forty-one thousand went out like a light, and a new line came up over the crucible.

**[Assaying…]**
**[Object: Ledger page (1).]**
**[Origin: —]**
**[Assaying…]**
**[Unable to appraise: Private record (Unregistered lender).]**
**[Assaying…]**

The crossbeam shuddered.

**[Unable to appraise.]**
**[Assaying…]**
**[Unable to appraise.]**
**[Assaying… Assaying… Assaying…]**

The draft stopped so suddenly that Bram fell forward onto one knee. Numbers started to pour out of the Assayer, gold numbers, hundreds, all over the room, on the bricks, on the walls, on us. *0. 7. 41,000. 2. 260. 0. 1,500. 12,057.* It was pricing everything it could reach, over and over, trying to find a scale big enough to fit one sheet of paper on, and there wasn't one.

The crucible glowed brighter. White, then a white that hurt to look at. And then, with a sound like a dinner plate dropped on tile, a crack opened down the front of it from the lip to the base.

Behind the crack something burned blue.

**[Weakness exposed: Assay Core.]**

"*Jonah!*"

He was already drawing. He'd been drawing, I think, since the numbers started. He let the breath out the way he'd done in the Kiln corridor four days ago, and the arrow went through the crack in the crucible and into the blue light without touching clay on either side.

The Assayer screamed. It wasn't a voice. It was every scale in every market on Earth slamming down at once.

Bram got up off his knee, took two steps, and drove the bottom edge of his tower shield into the crack with his whole weight behind it.

The crucible broke in half.

The pans dropped. The chains went slack. The crossbeam tipped all the way to the left, and stayed there, crooked, the way scales sit in a shop after closing.

**[The Assayer has been defeated.]**

---

For a while nobody did anything except breathe.

Then Nora walked over. She didn't run. She stopped beside me and looked down into the broken crucible, and I looked too.

The page was gone. Of course it was. The clay was full of white ash, still glowing at the edges, and for a moment that was all it was.

Then the ash settled, and it had settled into a shape. Letters. Thin and gray and crisp, like the impression a pen leaves on the sheet under the one you wrote on. Only one line had survived, and it had changed.

*#6 — Collection pending. Day 30.*

There hadn't been a date on the original. I'd read it a hundred times. There hadn't been a date.

Nora read it. I watched her read it. She didn't say anything. She reached down, fast, before I could stop her, and touched the ash with one finger, and the line didn't smear. It had hardened like glaze.

"Leave it," I said.

"I wasn't going to take it," she said, and stood up, and walked back to Owen without looking at me.

Jonah came over last. "Was that the page?"

"Yes."

"The one with my forty-one on the back."

"Yes. Sorry."

He thought about it for a second. "I remember it," he said. "That's enough. Don't need paper for that."

---

**[REGIONAL NOTICE]**
**[Spire, Floor 1 (The Kiln): FIRST CLEAR.]**
**[Party: Eli Marsh, Nora Marsh, Jonah Webb, Owen Park, Bram Okafor.]**
**[First Clear Bonus (Regional): 2,400 Merit — distributed by contribution.]**
**[Eli Marsh — Contribution: 30% — 720 Merit.]**

Five names in gold over the room. Somewhere outside, over the plaza, the same five names were hanging over the whole city. I didn't feel like celebrating, exactly. I felt like a man who'd just closed a very large account and was afraid to look at the fees.

**[Level Up! Appraiser Lv. 10… Lv. 11… Lv. 12]**

Nora's came up a second later. She didn't read it out. Owen did, from her face. "Thirteen," he said, and she nodded.

The Assayer left one thing behind, in the crooked right pan. A gray stone the size of a deck of cards, smooth on one side and scored with fine lines on the other.

**[Assayer's Touchstone — Market Value: 600 Merit | True Value: 1,500 Merit]**

"Private sale," I said. "Same as the core. If nobody minds."

Nobody minded.

**[Private Sale: Assayer's Touchstone — 20% of True Value = 300 Merit.]**
**[Title: Late Fee (+5% Boss drops) — 315 Merit.]**

"Three hundred fifteen," I said. "Split five ways. Sixty-three each. No odd Merit this time." I looked at Nora. "So your turn for the extra one waits."

"Great," Nora said. "Very exciting."

I did the rest of the math out loud, because I always do, and because I wanted to hear it in a room that wasn't trying to melt me.

"One thousand eight hundred thirty-two. Plus seven hundred twenty is two thousand five hundred fifty-two. Plus sixty-three."

**[Balance (Eli): 2,615 Merit]**

"Two thousand six hundred fifteen," I said. "Installment's two thousand. Due Day Thirteen, noon. That's six hundred fifteen over, with three days to spare."

"And Damon's hundred eighty on Day Twelve," Owen said, writing it on his clipboard.

"If he pays."

"You'll be over either way," Owen said. "For the first time." He underlined something twice.

Bram had sat down against the brick wall with his shield across his knees. "First clear," he said, to nobody in particular. "The Pact talked about first clear for a week. They had a speech written." He laughed once, quietly, and didn't say anything else.

---

We came out of the black door into evening.

The plaza was full. People had seen the notice. Someone started clapping and stopped when nobody joined in, and then about fifty people joined in at once, raggedly, the way a crowd does when it isn't sure if it's allowed. Somebody called Bram's name. Somebody called Nora's.

Owen and Jonah went ahead toward the hotel, with Bram behind them, clearing the crowd with his shield the way a ship clears water.

Nora walked beside me. She'd been quiet since the ash. She was holding her scorched sleeves in her fists.

Halfway across the plaza, by the message wall, she stopped walking.

I took two more steps before I noticed. I turned around.

She was looking at me the way she'd looked at the thermometer when she was small and I was sick: like the number was going to be bad, and she wanted to be the one to read it anyway.

"That page," she said. "I saw it burn. Before the ash. I saw the lines."

"Nora."

"Number six. *Collateral: Nora Marsh.* My name." Her voice was very level. "I've known that one since the library."

The crowd moved around us. Someone bumped my shoulder and said sorry.

"So tell me," my sister said. "Number seven. That's me too, isn't it?"
