# Chapter 2: Day Zero Pricing

She picked up on the fourth ring.

"Oh, so you *are* alive."

Her voice. I had to put my free hand on the stairwell railing.

"Nora. Listen to me."

"No, you listen. You said you'd call Thursday. It's Saturday. Do you know what Aunt Pam said to me? She said, 'Well, Eli's always been *private*.' In that voice. I had to defend you, Eli. I hate defending you."

"Nora, where are you right now?"

A pause. The tone of it changed. She'd always been able to hear things in my voice that I didn't know I was putting there. "My room. Why?"

"Which floor is your room on?"

"Second. Why? What's going on? Are you drunk? It's eleven fifty-two."

"I'm not drunk." I went down the stairs two at a time and shouldered out onto Callow Street. Bright, cold noon. A woman walking a dog. A kid on a scooter. The bakery on the corner had its door propped open and the whole block smelled like bread. It was the most normal street I had ever seen, and I wanted to scream at every single person on it. "In about eight minutes, something's going to happen. To the sky. It's going to look like the end of the world, and it's going to feel like you're dreaming. You're not dreaming. When it's over, you go up to the fourth floor. You find a room with a door that locks. You stay there. Don't go outside. Don't go near the river. Don't go down to the basement for any reason, even if people are screaming for help down there."

Silence on the line.

"Eli."

"I know how it sounds."

"You sound like you did after Mom."

I stopped walking.

"I'm not asking you to believe me," I said, carefully. "I'm asking you to do it anyway. Just this once. Do what I say, and on Sunday you can yell at me for as long as you want. In front of Aunt Pam. I'll stand there and take it."

Another pause. I could hear her breathing.

"Fourth floor," she said finally. "Locked door. No river. No basement."

"Promise me."

"I promise, you weirdo. Are you okay? Should I call someone?"

"I'm better than I've been in a long time," I said, and it was true, which was the strangest part. "I love you. I'll see you soon."

"Okay, now I'm *actually* worried—"

The clock on my phone flipped to 11:59. I hung up before I could hear the rest, because if I kept listening I'd never stop.

---

I didn't try to reach her.

I want to be honest about that, because I spent the first minute after the call trying to make it work. I stood on the corner with my phone open to a map and ran it every way I could. Twenty-five minutes by car, on a good day. At noon, every driver on Earth would be yanked out of their body and dropped into a white room, and every car would keep rolling without them. There'd be no roads by 12:05. On a bike, through wrecks and panic, it was an hour, minimum.

And at 12:15, the first tutorial monsters would spawn on the streets.

I knew that, too. In the old life, the first people to die on Day Zero weren't the weak ones. They were the fast ones. The people who ran for their families across the city before they'd picked a class or bought a single thing. Level zero, on foot, in the open. The Exchange ate them first.

I would have been one of them. I'd have been proud of it, too, right up until something with too many teeth caught me on a bridge.

So I stood on the corner of Callow and Fifth, a mile from anywhere that mattered, and I did the thing I was good at. I made a budget.

I had seventy-two hours and a debt of five hundred Merit. I had a skill that could see two prices where everyone else saw none. I had one key location memorized, in my own district, three blocks away.

And I had a Lender who had just shown me, whether he meant to or not, that the rules of this world could be bent if you paid the right person.

The sky turned gold.

---

It didn't happen slowly. There was no warning rumble, no wind. One moment the sky was blue, and the next it was the color of a new coin from horizon to horizon, and words were forming in it, a mile tall, while everyone on Callow Street stopped and looked up. The woman with the dog. The kid on the scooter. The baker, in the doorway, with flour on his hands.

**[GLOBAL NOTICE]**
**[Welcome to the Exchange.]**
**[Your world has been acquired.]**
**[Please stand by for onboarding.]**

The kid on the scooter said, "Whoa," in a small voice.

Then the world went white.

---

The onboarding room was exactly the way I remembered it, which somehow made it worse. A white space with no walls, no floor, no source of light, only a vague sense of being somewhere. A list hung in front of me like a restaurant menu.

**[Please select a Class.]**
**[Selection is permanent.]**

Warrior. Ranger. Elementalist. Cleric. Rogue. Artisan. A dozen more under those. And at the very bottom, in the same plain font as everything else, the one nobody took unless they were a coward, or a hoarder, or me.

**[Appraiser (Non-Combat)]**

Last time, I'd picked it in four seconds. I remembered the reasoning clearly. *It doesn't make you fight.* That was it. That was the whole thought. I'd been twenty-five, terrified, and ashamed of being terrified, and I'd spent the next seven years proving my reasoning right in the worst possible way.

This time, I had a second number.

**[Appraise]**

**[Warrior — Market Value: 120 Merit | True Value: 95 Merit]**
**[Elementalist — Market Value: 140 Merit | True Value: 160 Merit]**
**[Rogue — Market Value: 90 Merit | True Value: 70 Merit]**
**[Cleric — Market Value: 130 Merit | True Value: 210 Merit]**

I went down the list. Most classes sat close to their market price, a little under or a little over. The Exchange was a fair market, most of the time. That was what made it so hard to beat.

Then I reached the bottom.

**[Appraiser — Market Value: 5 Merit | True Value: 41,000 Merit]**
**[Condition: Ledger-Bound. Value realized only with an active private account.]**

I looked at that number for a long time.

Five, and forty-one thousand. The widest spread I'd ever seen on anything. The worst class on the board, and the one I was sitting on had eight thousand times more value than everyone thought, on one condition, and the condition was a faceless man with a chair and a pen.

I'd had the best class in the world for seven years. I'd just never had a buyer.

"Appraiser," I said.

**[Class Selected: Appraiser.]**
**[Starting Grant: 10 Merit.]**
**[Starter Shop is now open. Items purchased here will appear in your inventory.]**

---

The starter shop was a simple list, and everyone in the world was looking at their own copy of it right now, trying to decide between a sword and a healing potion. I had seen men get into fistfights on Day Two over who'd chosen right.

**[Minor Healing Draught — 5 Merit]**
**[Iron Shortsword — 8 Merit]**
**[Padded Vest — 6 Merit]**
**[Tallow Candle — 1 Merit]**
**[Rusted Iron Key — 1 Merit]**

Every Appraiser from the old life knew about the key. It was a joke on the forums in Year One, after the man who found the Market District vault posted about it. *The one-Merit item nobody bought.* By then it was gone from every shop.

**[Appraise]**

**[Rusted Iron Key — Market Value: 1 Merit | True Value: 380 Merit]**

I bought the key. I bought the healing draught. That left four Merit, and I kept it, because the one lesson I'd learned from seven years of watching adventurers go broke was that the last coin in your pocket is always the most expensive one.

**[Purchase complete. Returning you to your location.]**

---

Callow Street came back in pieces.

A car had rolled through the bakery's front window. Its driver sat behind the wheel with both hands in his lap and his mouth open, looking at nothing. The woman with the dog was sitting on the curb, holding the leash in both hands, shaking. The kid with the scooter was crying for his dad. Somewhere a few blocks over, a car alarm was going off. Then a second one. Then what sounded like a hundred.

I walked past all of it. That's another thing I want to be honest about.

I'd had seven years to get used to walking past people. I didn't like it. I just knew what happened to the ones who stopped.

**[Tutorial Zone: Fourteen — Market District]**
**[Zone Boundaries Seal In: 00:48:12]**
**[Objective: Survive 72 hours. Merit earned during the tutorial is retained.]**

At the corner of Fifth and Merchant, I passed the old courthouse. There were already a few dozen people gathering on its steps, looking for someone to tell them what to do.

And someone was.

He stood on the top step in a gray suit that had been expensive this morning. Tall, square shoulders, good hair, a smile that made you feel like you were already on the team. A thin steel sword hung at his belt, fresh from the starter shop. Damon Kreel, age twenty-seven, seven years younger than the last time I'd seen him walking out of a door.

He didn't have a guild yet. He didn't have forty people. He had a crowd of scared strangers and a voice that carried.

"—what matters is that we stick together," he was saying. "Whatever this is, it's got rules. Rules mean it can be figured out. Anyone who picked a fighting class, come stand with me. Anyone who didn't, that's fine. We'll need you too."

I should have kept walking. I knew that. I was on a budget.

Instead I stopped at the bottom of the steps, because I had spent seven years wanting to stand exactly here, knowing what I knew now.

Damon's eyes landed on me. They always did. He had a gift for picking out the useful people in a crowd, and for knowing exactly how useful.

"You," he said. "What'd you pick?"

"Appraiser."

A few people laughed, not unkindly. Damon didn't. He smiled wider, and I watched it happen, the same thing I'd watched happen seven years ago: the quick math behind his eyes, the moment he filed me under *tools.*

"That's great, actually," he said. "Someone's going to have to keep track of what we find. Split it fairly. Stick with me, and I'll make sure you're looked after."

Word for word. Seven years ago, I'd said *thank you.*

**[Appraise]**

**[Damon Kreel — Human — Warrior (Lv. 0)]**
**[Market Value: 14 Merit | True Value: 9 Merit]**

"Fourteen," I said.

He blinked. "Sorry?"

"You're worth fourteen Merit on the open market. Nine, if someone looked closely." I said it loudly enough that the people on the steps could hear, and I said it politely, the way you tell a man his watch is a fake. "I've got plans today that cost more than you. But thanks for the offer."

There was a short, ugly silence. Somebody on the steps snorted. Then somebody else laughed, for real this time, and it wasn't at me.

Damon's smile stayed exactly where it was. That was the scary thing about Damon. It always did.

"Good luck out there, then," he said.

"You too."

I walked on. My hands were shaking, and I let them. It was a small thing. It was a stupid thing. He didn't even know what he'd done yet, because he hadn't done it.

It still felt better than anything had in seven years.

---

The First Harbor Savings building sat two blocks down Merchant Row, a squat stone bank with bronze doors that had been old when my grandfather opened an account there. The doors stood half open. The security guard lay on the lobby floor next to his chair, very still. There was a dark smear on the marble leading away from him, toward the teller windows.

12:15. Right on schedule.

I heard it before I saw it. A soft, regular clicking, like someone counting coins onto a counter.

It came around the end of the teller line on four legs. About the size of a big dog, but thinner, and covered in something that looked like hammered copper instead of fur. Its head was mostly mouth. Where the eyes should have been, it had two round, flat discs, like coins set into the skull, and the clicking came from them, turning in their sockets.

**[Appraise]**

**[Tallyhound — Tutorial Beast (Lv. 2)]**
**[Market Value of Parts: Hide 4 Merit | Teeth 3 Merit | Left Eye 12 Merit | Right Eye 1 Merit]**

I read the numbers and felt something cold and happy settle in my chest.

Twelve and one. The eyes looked the same. They weren't. Everything that had value in this world had it for a reason, and there was only one reason a monster's left eye would be worth twelve times its right. That was where the core sat.

The Tallyhound saw me and started to run.

I didn't. I had a rusted key, a healing potion, and a level of zero. What I also had was seven years of watching men who did know how to fight, from the back of the room, where I'd had nothing to do but pay attention. I stepped sideways behind the end of the teller counter and snatched the brass letter opener off the nearest desk.

It came over the counter in a leap. I dropped flat. Its claws scraped the marble above my head, and it landed hard on the other side and skidded. Before it could turn, I rolled up onto my knees and drove the letter opener into its left eye with both hands and all my weight.

The disc cracked like cheap porcelain. Something behind it popped.

The Tallyhound made a sound like a cash register slamming shut, and fell over, and didn't get up.

**[Tallyhound defeated.]**
**[Merit Earned: 12]**
**[Level Up! Appraiser Lv. 1]**

I sat on the floor of the bank for a minute, breathing. My hands were covered in something that smelled like pennies.

Then I got up and went downstairs to the vault.

---

The safe deposit room was behind a steel gate that hung open, the lock shorted out by whatever had turned on the world. Rows of numbered boxes on every wall. I didn't need to look. The forum post had given a number, and I'd read it so many times in the Year One archives that I could see it with my eyes shut.

Box 114.

The rusted key slid into the lock like it had been cut for it, which I suppose it had.

**[Hidden Cache opened.]**
**[Contents: Gilded Compass. Merchant's Signet. Pouch of Merit (40).]**

**[Appraise]**

**[Gilded Compass — Market Value: 30 Merit | True Value: 220 Merit]**
**[Merchant's Signet — Market Value: 25 Merit | True Value: 150 Merit]**

Four Merit left over from the shop, plus forty, plus twelve from the hound. Fifty-six. And two items worth three hundred and seventy together, if I could find someone who knew what they were.

Which was the problem. Nobody knew. Not on Day Zero. On the open market, today, I'd get fifty-five for both of them, from someone who'd resell them for four times as much in a month.

Then a third window opened, in the old paper color.

**[Private Account — Services Available]**
**[Private Sale: The Lender will purchase items at 35% of True Value.]**
**[Limit: One (1) item per 12 hours.]**

Thirty-five percent of True Value. Not market. True.

I sold the signet.

**[Merchant's Signet sold. Merit Earned: 52]**
**[Balance: 108 Merit]**
**[First Installment: 500 Merit. Due: 70:41:19]**

One hundred and eight. Less than a quarter of what I needed, in the first hour and a half. If I could keep doing this every twelve hours, if the old forum posts were right about half of what they said, if the Lender didn't change the rules on me again, it was possible.

I kept the compass. It was worth too much to sell before I understood it.

I pulled up the tutorial map. It was a new feature I'd forgotten about, a thin gold overlay of the whole city, divided into numbered zones. My own zone glowed faintly. Fourteen. Sealed now, the boundary a thin shimmering line three blocks east.

I found Zone Nine at the bottom of the map, down by the river channel.

**[Zone Nine — The Drowned Campus]**
**[Tutorial Difficulty: ★★★★★ (Highest in Region)]**
**[Survivors: 1,204]**

I watched the number.

It went to 1,199. Then 1,196. Then, while I stood there with a dead monster's blood drying on my hands, it went to 1,187.

Fourth floor, I thought. Locked door. I told her the fourth floor.

The paper-colored window changed.

**[Private Service Available: Zone Transfer]**
**[Cost: 300 Merit]**
**[Current Balance: 108 Merit. Insufficient.]**
**[Finance the remainder (192 Merit)? Financed amount is added to your First Installment.]**
**[New First Installment if accepted: 692 Merit. Due: 70:40:02]**
**[Y / N]**

I stood in the vault and thought about it for exactly as long as it took the number to drop again.

1,183.

Six hundred and ninety-two Merit in seventy hours, or the Lender took her, and took her retroactively, and nothing I did in Zone Nine would count for anything. I'd be walking into the hardest tutorial zone in the region at level one with a letter opener and a debt I couldn't pay.

Last time, I'd stayed in the closet.

"Yes," I said.

The vault folded up around me like a piece of paper.

---

I landed in water.

Cold, black, waist-deep water, and the shock of it knocked the air out of me. I came up gasping in the dark. Something above me dripped steadily. Concrete walls. Pipes. A single emergency light, red, blinking somewhere far down a corridor, reflecting in the water in long wobbling lines.

A basement. I was in a basement.

*Don't go down to the basement for any reason.*

**[Zone Transfer complete. Welcome to Zone Nine: The Drowned Campus.]**

The water moved.

Not a ripple. A current. Slow and heavy, against my legs, the way the sea pulls at you when a big wave is coming in somewhere behind you. Something long was passing through the dark water a few meters away. I couldn't see it. I didn't need to.

My skill fired on its own, like a flinch.

**[Appraise]**

**[Hollow Matron — Zone Beast (Lv. ??)]**
**[Market Value: 2,000 Merit]**

It stopped moving.

Then, very slowly, the current turned around.
