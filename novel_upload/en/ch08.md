# Chapter 8: Callable

The Kiln opened at dawn on Day Six, and by seven in the morning, it had killed nineteen people.

I watched it happen the way everyone in the city did, on the board. The Spire had gone up in the middle of downtown on Day Zero, a white needle a hundred floors tall, and for six days its door had been sealed. At 05:00 it opened. At 05:10, the first raid party went in. They were a group from Zone Four who called themselves the Iron Wolves, and they'd spent the whole tutorial telling anyone who'd listen that they'd be first up the tower.

They were first.

**[REGIONAL NOTICE]**
**[Spire, Floor 1 (The Kiln): Party "Iron Wolves" — Wiped.]**

Two more parties went in before seven. The board announced both of them the same way. Then someone who'd gotten out alive posted a description on the public message wall next to the board, and the whole square read it at once. A corridor. Stone walls. Every ten minutes, the heat came up through the floor until the walls glowed orange and the air itself caught fire.

**[Emberleaf Salve — Last Sale: 3 Merit]**
**[Emberleaf Salve — Last Sale: 9 Merit]**
**[Emberleaf Salve — Last Sale: 22 Merit]**

I'd listed all eighty-six jars at midnight, at forty Merit each, and gone to sleep.

When I woke up, Nora was sitting on the end of my bed in the hotel room with her status window open, staring at it.

"Eli," she said. "Your jars are gone."

---

**[Emberleaf Salve (x86) — SOLD. Average price: 36 Merit.]**
**[Gross: 3,096 Merit. Exchange Fee (5%): 155 Merit.]**
**[Balance: 2,943 Merit]**

I read it four times. Then I read the name of the buyer on the last three lots, because the board listed buyers on large purchases, and I started laughing so hard I had to sit down on the floor.

**[Buyer: VANGUARD (Guild Account)]**

"What?" said Nora. "What's funny?"

"Damon bought the last thirty jars. At forty each."

"Is that good?"

"It's the best thing that's ever happened to me," I said, and wiped my eyes. "Including the pretzels."

She stared at me. Then, slowly, she smiled, and then she was laughing too, and for about a minute we were two idiots on a hotel floor on the sixth day of the end of the world, laughing at green paste.

Then she stopped.

"So you can pay," she said. "The installment. You've got enough."

"Yes."

"Then I don't need the bonus." She reached for the contract on the nightstand, the one she'd brought back from Merchant Row. "I'll go tell him I'm out."

"You can't," I said. "Read the penalty."

She'd read it. I'd shown her the night before, line by line, the way I'd shown her the loan. She'd gone very quiet and then spent a long time in the bathroom with the door shut. Now she looked down at the paper in her hand.

*Refusal constitutes breach. Forfeiture of all Merit and equipment, plus 5,000 Merit.*

"Five thousand," she said. "If I quit."

"And he'll call you up as Line Holder at noon today. The muster's at eleven on the courthouse steps. He's taking Vanguard into the Kiln at twelve." I looked at the clock on the wall. 08:14. "If you refuse, you're in breach. If you go, you stand at the front of a corridor that catches fire every ten minutes while thirty people run past you."

"So what do I do?"

I pulled up the board. Not the item listings. A different section, one that most people wouldn't look at for another month, because on Day Six nobody knew it existed.

**[PUBLIC EXCHANGE — CREDIT MARKET]**

"You don't do anything," I said. "I'm going shopping."

---

Here's something about the Exchange that took the world about a year to understand, the last time around.

It didn't just sell swords and potions and monster parts. It sold everything. That was the whole idea of it. It had bought a planet and put a price on every object and every skill and every monster on it, and it didn't see any reason to stop at objects. When the Exchange lent a new guild three thousand Merit to charter itself, it didn't keep the loan. It packaged it into a note and put the note up for sale on the Credit Market within forty-eight hours, for whoever wanted to buy it.

Almost nobody did, in the early days. A loan to a guild that hadn't done anything yet was a terrible investment. Most of the first wave of guilds died in the Spire and never paid a single Merit back.

**[Credit Note: Exchange Charter Credit — VANGUARD]**
**[Face Value: 3,000 Merit. Rate: 6% weekly.]**
**[Listed at: 1,150 Merit]**

The market thought Damon had about a one-in-three chance of surviving the week. That was fair. Three parties had already died in the Kiln this morning, and he was about to walk into it.

**[Appraise]**

**[Credit Note (VANGUARD) — Market Value: 1,150 Merit | True Value: 2,700 Merit]**
**[Covenant (1): Holder may review and void any Member Agreement funded by this Credit.]**
**[Covenant (2): If Guild membership falls below 16, the Credit becomes callable in full.]**

I read it twice. Then I read it a third time, very slowly, because I'd learned my lesson about the last four words.

The Charter Credit was three thousand. The charter had cost one thousand. Damon had used the other two thousand to fund signing bonuses. Nora's two thousand, most likely. Maybe a few others. That meant her contract was *funded by this credit.*

And whoever held the note could void it.

"How much is it?" said Nora, reading over my shoulder.

"Eleven hundred and fifty."

"That leaves you with—"

"Seventeen ninety-three." I didn't have to do the math. I'd done it already. "Two hundred and seven short of the installment. With seven days left."

"Eli, *no.*"

"Yes."

"You'll be short again! You'll be on another roof—"

"I'll have a guild paying me a hundred and eighty Merit a week in interest," I said. "And seven days. That's enough. I've done it on less." I looked at her. "You sold me the salve money without asking. On the roof, I sold something without telling you. We're even."

She glared at me. It was our mother's glare. I only knew that because Aunt Pam had told me once, a long time ago, at a funeral.

"This is so stupid," she said.

"You're the one who taught me about going rates."

**[Credit Note (VANGUARD) — PURCHASED.]**
**[Holder: Eli Marsh.]**
**[Balance: 1,793 Merit]**

---

At eleven o'clock, the Vanguard Guild mustered on the courthouse steps.

There were thirty-one of them. Gray armbands. Fresh gear bought on credit, from the look of it, polished and new and a little too big on some of them. Thirty of them carried a small green jar clipped to their belts.

A crowd had gathered to watch. After this morning's notices, anyone going into the Kiln was a spectacle. There were people selling bread and cups of water along the edge of the square, and the whole thing had the feeling of a sports game, or a public execution.

Damon stood on the top step, the same step as on Day Zero, in armor now instead of a gray suit. He looked good in it. He always had.

"Three parties went into the Kiln this morning," he said. His voice carried over the square without him needing to shout. "Three parties died. Not because they were weak. Because they were disorganized. They went in without a plan and without the right gear." He touched the jar on his own belt. "We have both. We have fire resistance for everyone. And we have something none of them had."

He looked out over the crowd until he found Nora, standing at the bottom of the steps next to me.

"We have the only Warden in the region who's held the line against a zone boss. Nora Marsh, you'll take the front. You'll hold the corridor while we push through. Everyone else, stay behind her and move fast."

A few people in the crowd clapped. It was a good speech. It had been a good speech on the hundredth floor too, when he'd said *Eli, I need your eyes on this.*

"No," said Nora.

The clapping stopped.

Damon smiled at her. "Sorry?"

"I said no." She didn't look at me. She held her chin up, the way she had at the library door. "I'm not going to hold your line."

"I understand you're nervous." His voice didn't change at all. "But I think if you check your agreement, you'll see that the Guild Master has the right to assign Line Holder. Refusing is a breach." He let that settle. "I'd hate for you to lose everything over a case of nerves. I really would."

"She's not in breach," I said, "because she doesn't have an agreement."

Every head on the steps turned toward me.

I walked up. Not fast. I'd been thinking about this walk for two lives, and I wasn't going to rush it. On the hundredth floor, he'd walked away from me through a door, and I'd watched his back until the ceiling came down. I'd pictured this so many times, in so many versions, that the real one felt strangely small. Just stone steps. Just a man in borrowed armor, and a lot of people watching, and my heart going far too fast. I stopped four steps below him, which put us about eye to eye, and I held my hand out flat and opened the window so it faced him. You couldn't show someone your status. But you could show them a notice, if you held it out.

**[Credit Note: Exchange Charter Credit — VANGUARD]**
**[Holder: Eli Marsh]**

Damon read it. I watched his eyes move. Left to right, then back to the start.

"I bought your charter this morning," I said. "From the Exchange. On the open market. It's all legal, you can check. Under Covenant One, the holder can review any member agreement funded by the credit." I turned my hand. A second window. "I've reviewed Nora Marsh's. It's void."

**[Member Agreement (Nora Marsh) — VOIDED by Credit Holder.]**

A murmur went through the crowd. Through the guild, too. Thirty-one people in gray armbands looking at each other.

"And while I was reviewing it," I said, louder now, "I found something interesting. Clause Two. *Guild Master may designate any member as Line Holder for any encounter.* That's not in the summary you gave out, is it? It's in the fine print. I'm an Appraiser, so I read fine print for a living. I'd guess it's in all of yours." I looked past Damon, at the guild. "If anybody wants to know, I'll read your contract for free. Right now. Just hold it out."

Nobody moved for a second.

Then a young woman at the edge of the group, a Ranger with a bow still wrapped in its shop paper, held out her agreement. Her hand was shaking.

**[Appraise]**

"Clause Two," I said. "Line Holder. It's there."

She lowered her hand. She looked at Damon.

Then the man next to her held out his. And the one behind him. And then there were a dozen hands in the air on the courthouse steps, a dozen folded contracts, and I read every one of them out loud, and every one of them said the same thing.

*Guild Master may designate any member as Line Holder.*

Any member. Any one of them could have been put at the front of the Kiln. Any one of them could have been left on the hundredth floor.

The Ranger took off her gray armband and dropped it on the step. That was all it took. They didn't run. They didn't shout. They just started walking away, one by one, the way people walk out of a bad restaurant, and the armbands lay on the stone behind them like leaves.

**[VANGUARD — Members: 31]**
**[VANGUARD — Members: 24]**
**[VANGUARD — Members: 15]**

**[Covenant (2) triggered: Membership below 16. Credit is now CALLABLE in full.]**

Damon was still standing on the top step.

For the first time in either of my lives, I watched his smile move. Not much. Just at the corners. Like a price tag starting to peel.

"You can't call three thousand Merit," he said quietly. "I don't have it. You'll get nothing."

"I know." I closed the windows. "So I'm not going to call it. Not today. You'll pay me the interest instead of paying the Exchange. A hundred and eighty a week, starting on Day Twelve. On time." I leaned in a little, so only he could hear the last part. "And you'll never say her name again. Not to recruit her, not to threaten her, not in a speech. If you do, I call the note, and Vanguard's charter belongs to me."

He looked at me for a long moment.

"Fourteen Merit," he said. "That's what you said I was worth."

"You've gone up," I said. "You're worth about three thousand now. You just don't own any of it."

I turned around and walked back down the steps. Nora was waiting at the bottom. She was trying very hard not to smile, and failing, and when I reached her she punched me in the shoulder, softer than in the dorm.

"You're so stupid," she said.

"You keep saying that."

"Because you keep doing it." She took my arm. "Come on. You still owe me two hundred and fifty Merit and forty dollars."

---

We didn't get far.

At the edge of the square, where the stalls were, a woman was waiting for us. She was maybe thirty, in a long gray coat, with short pale hair and the kind of calm face that I'd only ever seen on people who were very good at cards. She wasn't carrying a weapon. She didn't look like she'd ever needed one.

She was looking at me the way I looked at the board.

"That was well done," she said. "You read the covenant before anyone else in this city even knew there was a Credit Market."

"Thanks," I said. "Who are you?"

She didn't answer. She just looked at me a moment longer, and I felt it, the thing I'd felt from the wraith in the subway tunnel in Year One. A hand in my pocket. Somebody reading my price.

"*Eli Marsh,*" she said softly, as if she were reading a label. "*Appraiser. Lv. 9. True Value: Pledged. Seven accounts on file.*"

Nora's hand tightened on my arm.

Nobody else in the world could see True Value. That was the whole point of Ledger-Bound. It only worked with a private account. With a Lender.

"You're an Appraiser," I said.

"I'm a lot of things." She smiled, very slightly. "Right now, I'm an employee of the firm that bought your sixth loan. I'm not here to collect, Mr. Marsh. Not today. Collection on Loan Six is scheduled for Day Thirty."

She glanced at Nora then, just once, the way you glance at an item on a shelf that's already been reserved for someone else.

"I thought you'd want to know," she said, "before you spent any more of yourself trying to keep something that's already been sold."
