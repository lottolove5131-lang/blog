# Chapter 20: Called

The chains changed direction in the air.

Every one of them. They'd been coming out of the grille and going up the walls of the pit toward us, a dozen thin brass lines hunting for whoever owed, and when the bell rang under Nora's hand they stopped, all at once, like dogs hearing a whistle. Then they turned and went down.

To her.

**[THE TELLER: Line Holder identified. Party billing consolidated.]**
**[Sole debtor: Nora Marsh.]**
**[Estimated endurance: 04:10.]**

The first cycle hit her across the axe-haft she'd raised and she rocked back half a step and planted. The second came twelve seconds early, because a Line Holder doesn't get a billing cycle, she gets billed whenever the window feels like it. I watched her health bar go down in a clean, straight slice.

On our floor, nothing touched anyone.

Owen sat up. His bar was still red but it had stopped moving. He looked at his hands, and then over the lip of the pit, and I saw him understand, and I saw what it did to his face.

"Up," I said. "Owen, up. Heal yourself and hit the drawer. Everybody hit the drawer."

**[Till integrity: 96%.]**

---

We hit the drawer.

Bram hit it like he was trying to get through it to her. Jonah stopped shooting golems and put arrow after arrow into the keyhole. Owen healed himself with one hand and swung his mace with the other. I worked the hinges with my dagger and read out the golems' re-forming timers, because that was the only thing I was actually good for. Twenty seconds. Fifteen. *Left, Bram, it's standing up on your left.*

**[Till integrity: 91%.]**

**[Nora Marsh — 03:02 remaining.]**

Eighty-nine. Eighty-seven.

I did it in my head while I worked, the way I do everything. Five points in a minute, with four of us and no bills. Eighty-seven percent left. Seventeen minutes, give or take. She had three.

Behind us, Damon's fifteen stood against the door in a tight gray knot and didn't move. A few of them were looking at the pit. The Ranger who'd taken the chain earlier was sitting against the wall holding her arm. Damon stood in front of them with his hands behind his back.

"You can stop," he said to me. "It's done. She'll go to about ten percent and the window will close the account for the cycle. She'll live. Ashgrave doesn't want her dead, Eli. Dead collateral's worth nothing." He shrugged. "Damaged collateral is a different line item."

I didn't look at him.

I looked at the gold text that was still hanging over the grille. I'd read it when we came in and I'd been angry about it, and then the bell had rung and I'd stopped reading.

**[ENTRY REGISTERED: VANGUARD (Guild Account), represented by Damon Kreel.]**
**[Accompanying: Eli Marsh +4.]**

*Billing follows the party. A designated Line Holder is billed as sole debtor for the party.*

A designated Line Holder.

Not a Warden who rang a bell. *Designated.* The window was a teller, and tellers don't care about skills. Tellers care about paper.

And the party, as far as the paper was concerned, was Vanguard.

---

I stood up from the drawer.

"Eli?" Jonah said. "Eli, where are you—"

"Keep hitting it."

I walked across the marble to Damon Kreel. Not fast. I'd learned that on the courthouse steps. When you've got the paper, you don't run.

I held out my hand, flat, and opened the window so it faced him.

**[Credit Note: Exchange Charter Credit — VANGUARD]**
**[Holder: Eli Marsh]**
**[Status: CALLABLE in full (Covenant 2).]**

"No," Damon said.

"I'm calling it."

"You can't get anything. I told you on Day Six. I don't have three thousand Merit. I don't have three hundred." The smile was still there, but it had gone thin, like paint over a crack. "You'll get nothing."

"I know," I said. "I read Covenant Three."

He hadn't. I watched him not have read it.

**[Credit Note (VANGUARD) — CALLED by Holder.]**
**[Amount due: 3,000 Merit. Demand period: 00:00:10.]**
**[Covenant (3): If a called Credit is not repaid on demand, the Holder may assume the Guild Charter. The former Guild Master remains a founding member under the Guild's standard Member Agreement.]**

Ten seconds. He opened his own status. I watched his eyes go across it, left to right, and back. I could have told him what was there. I'd appraised him in the doorway.

**[00:00:03]**

**[00:00:00]**

**[Credit Note (VANGUARD) — DEFAULT.]**
**[Holder may assume the Guild Charter. Assume? Y / N]**

"Yes," I said.

**[GUILD CHARTER: VANGUARD]**
**[Guild Master: Eli Marsh.]**

Up on the wall above the door, the gold registration rewrote itself.

**[ENTRY REGISTERED: VANGUARD (Guild Account), represented by Eli Marsh.]**

---

The fifteen behind Damon looked at their wrists. Their armbands had changed color at the edge, a thin gold thread where the white V was. Mine had appeared on my sleeve without my asking, which I didn't like at all.

Damon understood before anyone else. He'd written the agreement. He knew exactly what was in it.

"Clause Two," I said. "You remember Clause Two. You stood on the courthouse steps and told my sister the Guild Master has the right to assign Line Holder. You said refusing was a breach. You said you'd hate for her to lose everything over a case of nerves."

"Eli."

"*Guild Master may designate any member as Line Holder for any encounter.*" I said it the way I'd read it to thirty-one people, slowly, so it would land. "Any member. That's you, now. You're a founding member. You signed the same paper they did. You just never thought anyone would read yours."

"You won't," Damon said. His voice was very low. "You're not that kind of man. You never were. That's why you were—"

He stopped.

I finished it for him.

"You were always the cheapest thing in the room, Damon." I said it quietly, the way he'd said it to me on the hundredth floor, with the door already closing behind him. "No offense."

**[Guild Master Eli Marsh designates: Damon Kreel — Line Holder (current encounter).]**
**[Refusal constitutes breach. Forfeiture of all Merit and equipment, plus 5,000 Merit.]**

The pit lit up gold.

**[THE TELLER: Designation on file supersedes skill. Contract outranks skill.]**
**[Nora Marsh — Hold the Line: RELEASED by senior designation.]**
**[Sole debtor: Damon Kreel.]**

The chains came up out of the pit.

They came up the walls in a rush, all of them, and they found Damon where he stood and wrapped his arms and his chest and his ankles, gently, like ribbons on a parcel. He didn't fight them. I'll give him that. He looked at me the whole time, and his face did nothing at all, and then the chains pulled, and he went over the lip and down eight meters into the pit, and landed on his feet in front of the window, because he was a Warrior at level fifteen and his armor was the good set.

The bell rang.

He took the bill on his chest and his health bar dropped a slice, and he stayed standing. The next one he tried to pay. I saw the window open in front of him, saw him push something into it, and saw the window take it and ring for more. Whatever Damon Kreel had left in his account, it was less than four hundred. The chains took the difference.

He drew his sword. It was the only thing he could do.

---

"Rope," I said. "Owen, rope. Bram—"

Bram was already over the edge. He didn't wait for rope. He went down the brass column hand over hand like he'd been born on a ship, dropped the last three meters, and landed beside my sister with his shield up between her and the window, and she sagged against the back of it like a door shutting.

"Two shields and an axe," I heard him say. "That's the formation. I told you. You don't stand alone, I don't stand alone. That was the deal."

"You weren't *here*."

"I'm here now."

I turned to the fifteen.

They were standing against the door, looking at me, and at the gold thread on their armbands, and at the pit where the man who'd hired them was taking bills alone. They looked scared. They looked like people who'd been told on Day Six that they were disposable and had stayed anyway, because staying was cheaper than starting over.

"I'm your guild master for about the next ten minutes," I said. "After that I'm going to void every one of your agreements, and you can go wherever you want. But right now there's a drawer, and it bills my sister if it doesn't break, and he won't hold forever." I pointed. "Those were your packs on her floor. Hit the drawer."

Nobody moved for a second. It was the courthouse steps again, the same held breath.

"He told us to stand still," a man near the door said. He was young, with a cut over one eye and a pack strap still over his shoulder, empty now. "He said she'd step up and we'd walk out. He said it was in the file."

"It was," I said. "Page one. You were the weight."

The Ranger was the first. She got up off the wall with her bad arm tucked in and drew her bow left-handed. "I'm not anybody's weight," she said, to no one in particular. Then the man with the cut. Then all of them.

Fifteen people hit the Till.

**[Till integrity: 71%.]**

**[Till integrity: 48%.]**

The golems stood up out of their coins and the fifteen knocked them back down before they'd finished, and Jonah stood on the lip of the pit and called Damon's health out loud, because nobody else would. "Sixty. Fifty-five. He's moving. He's okay. He's—fifty."

**[Till integrity: 19%.]**

Owen hit the hinge I'd been working on with his mace, two-handed, and the hinge gave.

**[Till integrity: 0%. Issuance ended.]**

The drawer came out of the brass column and fell open, and about four thousand gold coins poured out across the marble like a slot machine paying off, and every one of them was worthless. I knew without appraising. They were the bills nobody had paid.

Down in the pit, the Teller straightened up behind the grille with nothing left to write, and came out from behind its counter for the first time, and it turned out it couldn't fight. It was a teller. It swung its brass scoops at Damon and Bram and Nora like a clerk swatting flies, and Damon, who still had a sword and a reason, cut it in half at the waist.

The far pan rose. Slow, level. It met our floor with a soft chime, and the bronze seam closed, and the vault was one room again.

---

**[REGIONAL NOTICE]**
**[Spire, Floor 2 (The Counting House): Account settled. CLEARED.]**
**[First Clear: Eli Marsh, Nora Marsh, Jonah Webb, Owen Park, Bram Okafor + VANGUARD (15).]**

Nora was sitting on the marble with Owen's hands on her ribs. Her left arm was splinted with a strip of brass Owen had pried off the drawer. There was a gray mark across her armor, from collarbone to hip, where the chains had taken their four hundreds, and it didn't wash out when Owen poured water on it. Her health bar sat at eleven percent and climbed one point at a time under his hands.

"Three minutes," she said when I knelt next to her. Her voice was rough. "I did three minutes."

"You rang the bell."

"You were going to let Owen die arguing with me about a rule." She tried to sit up straighter and gave up. "And then you did that. With Damon. You read his paper at him."

"I did."

"Was that the plan the whole time?"

"No," I said. "I found it at ninety-one percent."

She looked at me for a long moment. Then she laughed, once, and it hurt her, and she did it anyway.

"You're so stupid," she said.

"You held the line."

"I know." She closed her eyes. "Don't. Not today. Shout at me tomorrow."

---

I did the paperwork standing in a pile of worthless coins.

**[Member Agreements (VANGUARD), x15 — VOIDED by Credit Holder (Covenant 1).]**

The gold thread vanished from fifteen armbands. One by one, they took them off. Nobody threw theirs on the floor this time. The Ranger folded hers and put it in her pocket.

**[GUILD CHARTER: VANGUARD — Status: Dormant.]**
**[Guild assets transferred to Holder: shared equipment (6 packs), headquarters lease (Merchant Row Hotel).]**

Damon came up out of the pit last, under his own power, with his health at about a third. He stood in front of me. His armor was scored gray in a dozen places. His sword was in his hand.

**[Line Holder designation: FULFILLED. No breach.]**
**[Outstanding: Charter Credit balance 3,000 Merit — Damon Kreel, personal, unsecured.]**

"You did the job," I said. "So nothing's forfeit. The packs and the hotel were Vanguard's. Those are mine. The sword's yours. The three thousand is yours too."

"You'll never see it."

"I know." I closed the window. "I'm not going to ask."

He looked at me the way he'd looked at me on the hundredth floor, as if I were a figure on the wrong side of a balance. Then he sheathed his sword, turned around, and walked to the far door, where the stairs went down, and went down them alone. Nobody went after him. Fifteen people who'd worn his armband an hour ago watched him go and didn't say anything.

---

The rewards came while Owen was still working on Nora.

**[First Clear Bonus (Regional): 4,000 Merit. Contribution: Eli Marsh — 25%.]**
**[+1,000 Merit.]**

**[Teller's Scales — Market Value: 700 Merit | True Value: 2,000 Merit]**

I sold them through the private sale, because that was the rule and because the Lender's rate was still better than the board's. Twenty percent of two thousand was four hundred. The Late Fee title bumped it five percent. Four hundred twenty.

"Equal fifths," I said out loud, because Bram was listening. "Four twenty is eighty-four each. No odd Merit this time. Nora's still next in line for the extra."

"Write it down," Nora said with her eyes closed. "I'm going to want that one."

Owen had been keeping the Clipboard House money in his own column all week, two hundred so far, and he moved it across without asking while I was still reading the notice.

**[Balance (Eli): 1,575 + 1,000 + 84 + 200 = 2,859 Merit]**
**[Eli Marsh — Appraiser — Lv. 15]**
**[Nora Marsh — Warden — Lv. 17]**

Two thousand eight hundred fifty-nine, three days before an installment of thirty-five hundred. Six hundred and forty-one short. I'd been further short with less time. I wrote it in the ledger, and turned it so Nora could see, because that was the deal now.

---

Jonah had gone down the stairs to check the landing. Old habit from the Kiln: nobody leaves a floor until someone's looked at what's waiting below it.

He came back up fast and quiet, two steps at a time. He crouched next to me and didn't look at Nora.

"Damon's at the bottom," he said, very low. "With the gray-coat woman. Vesper. I heard him."

"Heard what?"

Jonah's jaw worked.

"He said, *The collateral used Hold the Line. The impairment's on record, right?*"
