# Chapter 24: Appraisal Duel

By eleven-thirty on Day Twenty-Seven, there were more people in the courthouse plaza than there had been for Damon's muster on Day Six.

They stood on the steps, on the lip of the fountain, on the roofs of the stalls. Somebody had climbed the lamppost by the board and was sitting on the crossbar with his legs hanging down. The water sellers had come back, and the bread sellers, and a man selling paper cones of roasted chestnuts out of a steel drum, and the smoke from the drum drifted over everything and made the whole square smell like a holiday.

It had been a public execution and a sports game on Day Six. Today it felt more like the second one. People were betting. I could hear them.

"Ashgrave, three to one."

"He's got nothing. He's got a box."

He was right. I had a box. Owen was carrying it, with the rubber-banded stack of paper on top, and Owen's face was the face of a man walking into an exam he'd studied for all night.

The board had changed. At noon it would go dark from the top down, all the listings, all the bids, and the whole ten meters of glass would become one window. For now it showed a single line.

**[HEARING — Appraisal Objection. Commences: 00:28:14]**

Vesper was already on the steps. Third from the top, on the left. She had nothing with her. No case, no papers, no assistant. Just the gray coat and her hands in its pockets.

Damon was in the crowd. I found him without looking, the way you find a sore tooth with your tongue. Near the back, by the chestnut drum, in plain clothes, with the gray cord around his wrist that nobody had put there but him.

I went up the steps and stood third from the top, on the right.

Nora didn't come up. She couldn't.

At 11:58, a circle of gold light drew itself on the paving stones at the bottom of the steps, a meter and a half across, and a line of text hung over it.

**[Collateral shall stand within view of the Board.]**

Nora looked at it. Then she looked up at me. Then she stepped into it, the way she'd stepped into the Kiln, chin up, and stood there in the middle of the square with a thousand people looking at her and not one of them allowed to ask her anything.

At 12:00, the board went dark from the top down.

---

**[APPRAISAL OBJECTION — Eli Marsh v. Ashgrave Recoveries]**
**[Respondent's Appraiser will appraise first.]**

Vesper didn't step forward. She didn't need to. She took one hand out of her pocket and turned it, palm up, toward the circle, the way you'd show someone where the door is.

The board filled with gray.

**[Collateral: Nora Marsh — Warden (Lv. 17)]**
**[Event, Day 20: Hold the Line used. Spire Floor 2 (The Teller). Solo engagement: 3 min.]**
**[Injuries sustained: left arm, ribs. Status: healed.]**
**[Residual: Hold the Line resistance, −8%. Permanent.]**
**[Durability: 92%.]**
**[Collateral Valuation (Day 27): 6,400 Merit.]**
**[Recorded High (Day 19): 7,900 Merit.]**
**[Impairment: −1,500 Merit (−19%).]**

The crowd read it all at once. You could hear them do it. A thousand people breathing in.

"The Collateral was deployed alone against a floor boss," Vesper said. Her voice carried the way Damon's had, without effort, except that his had always sounded like a speech and hers sounded like a weather report. "She held for three minutes. She was hurt. She healed. But the skill she used, the skill that is most of her value as a Warden, came back eight percent weaker, and it will not come back further. That is the definition of impairment. The Holder accelerated collection. The Holder was entitled to." She put her hand back in her pocket. "That's all."

That was all. It was clean. It was short. Every number was true. I'd have written it the same way.

Seven thousand nine hundred minus six thousand four hundred was one thousand five hundred. Fifteen hundred over seventy-nine hundred was nineteen percent, near enough. She hadn't rounded in her own favor. She hadn't needed to.

At the side of the board, a thin gold bar appeared, like the balance on a scale.

**[Board Assessment (Provisional): Respondent.]**

It tipped left. Toward Vesper.

Somebody near the fountain said, "Damaged goods," not loudly. Someone laughed.

Down in the circle, Nora didn't move. She'd heard it. She was standing very straight, the way she did when somebody at the stall tried to haggle her below a fair price, and I knew that if she'd been allowed to speak, she would have said something that got us both thrown off the steps.

**[Objector's Appraiser may proceed.]**

---

I didn't appraise Nora.

I'd appraised her every morning for twenty-four days. I knew what her window said. Vesper had just shown the whole city, and arguing with it would be arguing with the weather.

"Section twenty-two," I said, to the board. My voice came out thinner than hers. I made it louder. "Filed instruments relating to the collateral are unsealed at the hearing. I'd like to see them."

A pause. Then a second window opened next to Vesper's, in Ashgrave's gray, and the board said:

**[Filed Instruments (1). Unsealed.]**

"Thank you," I said, because it seemed like the kind of thing you said to a court, and I held my hand out toward it.

**[Appraise]**

**[Instrument: Assignment of Loan 6. Private Lender to Ashgrave Recoveries.]**
**[Date of Purchase: Day 4, 00:00.]**
**[Price Paid: 600 Merit.]**
**[Terms: Collateral purchased AS IS.]**
**[Acceleration (per §14): permitted only upon a decline in Collateral value relative to the Date of Purchase.]**

I read it out loud. All of it. Slowly. The same way I'd read Clause Two on these steps, to a Ranger holding out her contract with a shaking hand.

"As is," I said. "Ashgrave bought my sister as is. For six hundred Merit. At midnight on Day Four, the minute the markets opened."

The crowd went quiet. Not all at once. In patches, the way a room goes quiet when the people at the front stop talking and the people at the back want to know why.

"And here." I pointed. "*Relative to the Date of Purchase.* Not relative to Day Nineteen. Not relative to her best day. Relative to the day they paid for her." I looked across the steps at Vesper. "Day Nineteen isn't in your contract, Ms. Kaine."

"No," she said. "It isn't."

She didn't argue. She didn't look surprised. She just watched me, the way she watched everything.

"So I'd like to ask the Board one question." I turned back to the glass. "What was the Collateral worth at midnight on Day Four?"

The board thought about it. The Exchange kept every price. That was the thing nobody understood about it, at the start. It never forgot what anything had cost.

**[Collateral Valuation (Day 4, 00:00): Nora Marsh — Warden (Lv. 10). 2,150 Merit.]**

"Two thousand one hundred and fifty," I said. "And today, by their own appraiser's number, six thousand four hundred."

I did it out loud, because I always did, and because I wanted every person on the lamppost to hear it.

"Six thousand four hundred minus two thousand one hundred and fifty. Four thousand two hundred and fifty. Not down. Up." I let it sit. "Nearly three times what they paid for. That's not impairment. That's the best trade Ashgrave ever made, and they're trying to collect early on it because she got hurt doing the job that made her worth it."

The gold bar at the side of the board shivered. It didn't move. Not yet.

---

"Owen," I said.

He came up the steps with the box. He didn't hand it to me. He took the top sheet off the stack and held it up to the board, flat, the way the Vanguard had held up their contracts on Day Six, and I appraised it as he held it.

"These are our books," I said. "Clipboard House. Every trade since Day Fourteen. Owen writes everything down. Ask anybody." I turned to the board. "I'd like to put a table in front of you."

I'd built it on the hotel stairs at two in the morning, on the back of a stall receipt. Owen had copied it out clean. He held it up now, and the board read it, and drew it in gold the size of a house.

**[Collateral (Nora Marsh): Day 4 / Day 27]**
**[Level: 10 / 17]**
**[Spire Floors first-cleared: 0 / 2 (Floor 1, Day 10; Floor 2, Day 20)]**
**[Regional Notices naming Collateral, since purchase: 4]**
**[Valuation: 2,150 / 6,400 (Respondent's figure)]**

I read it line by line. On every line, the crowd made a small noise, like someone being told a price they didn't expect at a market stall.

"Their number," I said, at the last line. "Not mine. I didn't appraise her today. I didn't need to."

Vesper took her hand out of her pocket.

"The residual is permanent," she said. "Eight percent off the line. A buyer in the market discounts for that. My valuation is honest, Mr. Marsh, but it is a valuation of a damaged asset. You're asking the Board to pretend a buyer wouldn't care."

It was a good answer. It was the right answer. The bar tipped back toward her, just a hair.

"Then let's ask the buyers," I said.

Owen took the next sheet off the stack.

"Day Five," he read. "Vanguard Guild. Signing bonus offered to Nora Marsh, Warden, Level Ten: two thousand Merit." He looked up. "That was before the Spire. Before she was hurt."

**[Verified: Vanguard Charter Credit disbursement, Day 5. 2,000 Merit.]**

Somewhere at the back, by the chestnut drum, somebody shifted. I didn't look.

"Day Twenty-Three," Owen read. "Three days after Floor Two. Three days after Hold the Line. A recruitment offer, forwarded through Clipboard House. Guild from Zone Four. Five thousand five hundred."

A man in the crowd raised his hand. The recruiter, the one who'd come to the hotel door. "That's mine," he said, loudly. "I made it. I'd make it again."

**[Verified: Bid notice, Day 23. 5,500 Merit.]**

"Day Twenty-Five," Owen read. "Six thousand. Day Twenty-Six." He turned the sheet. "Seven thousand two hundred."

**[Verified.]**
**[Verified.]**

"All after Day Twenty," I said. "All after the injury. Everybody who bid knew about the eight percent. It was in a regional notice. The whole city saw her come back down the Spire." I looked down at the circle. "The market saw the damage, and it bid *more.*"

"Your offers were made to your own sister," said Vesper, quite calmly, "and logged by your own business partner."

"Then ask whether my business partner lies."

That was when the student stepped out of the crowd.

He was skinny and nervous and he kept his hands in his pockets, like he had at the hotel door. He climbed two steps, which was as far as he dared.

"Day Fourteen," he said. "I brought a silt leech core to their stall. I was going to sell it for three Merit to the guy at the next table. He," and he pointed at me, "told me it was worth six. He could've bought it for three himself. He told me six and charged me two percent of nothing." He swallowed. "It's on the clipboard. Look it up."

Owen found the sheet. He didn't have to look long.

**[Verified: Clipboard House ledger, Day 14. Silt Leech Core, appraised 6. Fee: 0.]**

Then the heavy Warrior came forward, Damon's old second shield, the one who'd sat on a crate behind me for three days and said one word. He didn't climb the steps. He stood at the edge of the circle, next to Nora, and spoke up at the board.

"I was on Floor Two," he said. "With Vanguard. She held the Teller alone for three minutes. Then she got back up on her own." He looked up at Vesper. "I'd follow her line. Write that down. I'd pay to."

The crowd made a sound then that wasn't a murmur anymore.

---

I closed it the way I'd close a trade. Short.

"Ashgrave bought her as is," I said. "For six hundred Merit, on Day Four, when she was a Level Ten Warden with one zone boss to her name. The contract says they can only collect early if she's worth less than that. Their own appraiser says she's worth six thousand four hundred. The market says more. There's no decline. There's a rise of four thousand two hundred and fifty Merit." I looked at the board. "That's all."

The gold bar at the side of the glass swung right, hard, like a scale with a weight dropped on it.

**[RULING — Appraisal Objection]**
**[Basis for Acceleration: not established. Collateral value relative to Date of Purchase: +4,250 Merit.]**
**[Acceleration: VOID.]**
**[Collection Schedule restored: Day 30, 12:00.]**
**[Objector's Appraisal skill: retained.]**

For a second, nothing.

Then the plaza went up. Not cheering, exactly. It was the noise a market makes when a price nobody believed in turns out to be real. The man on the lamppost was shouting something. The student had his hands out of his pockets. Somebody near the fountain, maybe the same somebody who'd said *damaged goods*, was very quiet.

The gold circle around Nora went out.

She didn't move for a moment. She stood where it had been, looking down at the stones, like she wasn't sure it was really gone. Then she came up the steps two at a time and stopped one below me.

"Six thousand four hundred," she said.

"Their number."

"I'm worth more than Damon's whole guild." Her voice cracked a little at the end, and she covered it by punching me in the shoulder. "I wasn't even allowed to talk."

"You didn't need to," I said. "You were the evidence."

---

I looked for Damon.

The chestnut drum was still smoking. The space beside it was empty. People were moving through it, laughing, and none of them was him. I appraised the crowd out of habit and got a hundred windows of a hundred strangers and not one Damon Kreel.

Vesper was still on her step.

She'd watched the whole thing with the same calm face she'd had on Day Six, and when I came over she smiled. A real one. Small, and a little tired.

"You read the contract," she said. "Most people read the collateral."

"You picked Day Nineteen."

"I did."

"An appraiser like you doesn't pick the wrong baseline by accident."

She looked at me for a long moment. Then she looked down at the plaza, at Nora, at the fifteen, at Owen putting his sheets back in the box one by one, in order.

"I appraised what I was told to appraise, Mr. Marsh," she said. "That's what we do, at Ashgrave."

She went down the steps and into the crowd, and I lost her the way I always did, between one second and the next.

I still don't know whether she lost on purpose.

---

That night, the hotel was loud until nearly three. The fifteen had bought a keg of something from a stall by the board, and Jonah had got Bram to sing, which nobody had known was possible, and Owen fell asleep at the lobby table with his head on the box.

Nora had gone up hours before. I went up a little after three, Day Twenty-Eight already by the clock, because I wanted to look at the lien one more time before I slept. Day Thirty. Two more days. The schedule was back where it belonged.

I opened the door.

Nora was asleep in the other bed with her boots still on. And between the two beds, there was a chair.

It hadn't been there that morning. It was an ordinary wooden chair, the kind you'd find in any office, except that it was the color of old paper, the exact yellow of a window I'd been reading since the hundredth floor. And sitting in it, legs crossed, ledger on his knee, was a man with no face.

"Congratulations, Mr. Marsh," said the Lender. "That was very well done. I don't often get to watch a man win with someone else's contract."

I didn't close the door. I didn't go in.

He turned a page.

"I'd like to make you an offer," he said. "Wouldn't you like to see who's standing on the other side of the door, Mr. Marsh?" The blank oval of his face tilted toward me. "No charge. Just this once."
