# Chapter 5: Overdue

I stood in front of the door for too long.

Behind me, the second boat bumped against the steps, and then the third, and people started climbing out, whispering, their shoes squeaking on the wet stone. Nora came up beside me and read the carving. I watched her read it.

"Who's E?" she said.

"I don't know."

"It looks like your writing."

"Lots of people have bad handwriting."

"Eli." She didn't raise her voice. "Who else would write *her?* There's one Warden here. It's me."

I didn't have an answer that wasn't insane, so I didn't give one. I looked at the words again. The waterline they were cut into was old. Not two days old. Old like the rings on a tree stump, faded and gray, and the edges of the letters had softened the way cuts in wood soften over years.

*Year Nine, Day Three,* said the ledger in my head. *Priority: senior to all later claims.*

"Somebody's been here before," I said finally. "Somebody who knew what's inside. They're telling us Hold the Line gets you killed in there."

"So I don't use it."

"Then the Librarian goes after whoever's closest," I said. "And that's Owen. Or me. Or any of them." I didn't look back at the students crowding up behind us, because I didn't want to see their faces while I did this math. "You're the only one who can take a hit. Everyone else is level three or four. The Clerics are level two."

"So what do we do?"

I thought about the closet. I thought about the note, written by someone with my handwriting who'd stood here, maybe, and seen what I was about to see.

"We go in," I said, "and I watch the numbers."

---

The inside of Hale Memorial Library was dry.

That was the first wrong thing. Outside, the lake lapped against the third step. In here, the marble floor was dusty, and the air smelled of old paper and floor wax, and the reading lamps on the long tables all glowed with a soft green light. Rows of tall shelves ran back into the dark. It was so quiet I could hear my own pulse.

The second wrong thing was the wet footprints.

They came from somewhere deep in the stacks and led up to the circulation desk at the center of the room, and they were bare, and long, and there were far too many toes.

The doors shut behind us.

**[Zone Boss Encounter: The Drowned Librarian]**

It stood up from behind the desk.

It was very tall and very thin, and it was made of paper. Wet paper, layered and folded over and over itself like papier-mâché, gray and swollen and dripping. Pages hung off its arms like the sleeves of a robe. Its face was a single sheet of paper pressed over a skull that wasn't there, and through it I could see the ghost of printed text, lines and lines of it, moving. It had a stamp in one hand, a big old-fashioned rubber date stamp, and in the other hand it held a book.

It looked at us. Then it looked at the book. Then it tilted its paper head, very politely, the way a real librarian does when you're talking too loudly.

"Oh no," Owen whispered.

**[Appraise]**

**[The Drowned Librarian — Zone Boss (Lv. 24)]**
**[Market Value of Parts: Pages 1 Merit each | Stamp 40 Merit | Spine 400 Merit]**
**[Behavior: Issues loans. Collects on overdue items.]**

Issues loans.

For a second, I almost laughed. Of course. Of course that was the boss.

The Librarian stepped around the desk and walked toward us, in no hurry, and held out the book to the nearest person. It was one of the Artisans, a thin girl with a braid. She stared at the book. She didn't take it.

It didn't matter. The Librarian reached out, very gently, and pressed the date stamp against the back of her hand.

*Thunk.*

The book appeared in her arms. She screamed and tried to drop it, and it wouldn't drop. Her fingers were locked around it.

**[Appraise]**

**[Book: "Tidal Tables, Vol. III" — Checked out to: Maya Reyes]**
**[Due: 00:01:30]**
**[Overdue Penalty: Drowning.]**

"Everybody spread out!" I shouted. "Don't let it touch you!"

---

It turned out that you could not, in fact, avoid being touched by a seven-foot paper monster in a library full of dead ends.

In the first minute, it stamped four people.

It didn't attack. It didn't hurry. It just moved through the reading room with its long wet steps, pressing that stamp to arms and necks and hands, and every time it did, a book appeared, locked in someone's grip, with a timer on it that only I could read. The Warriors swung at it. Their swords went into the paper and came out wet and did nothing. The rugby shirt got a good hit into its side, and a clump of pages tore away and fell to the floor, and more pages folded in to fill the gap.

**[Maya Reyes — Due: 00:00:41]**

Maya had stopped screaming. She was on her knees next to one of the long tables, holding the book against her chest, breathing in short, sharp gasps. There was water coming out of the corner of her mouth. Just a little. Just a trickle.

*Overdue penalty: drowning.*

And it said *checked out.* Things that are checked out can be returned.

I looked at the circulation desk. At the center of it, under a brass sign, was a wide wooden slot.

**[RETURNS]**

"Maya!" I ran to her. I didn't think about it. I grabbed the book and the book didn't let go of her hands, so I grabbed her by both wrists and hauled her upright and dragged her across the marble toward the desk. She was heavier than she looked, and she was choking, and I was level five, which isn't much stronger than zero. "Put it in the slot! Put it in—"

She pushed the book into the return slot with both hands.

It went in. Her fingers came free. She fell back against the desk and threw up a mouthful of lake water onto the floor, and then she took a huge, whooping breath of air, and started crying.

**[Book returned. Loan cleared.]**

The Librarian turned its paper face toward us. For a moment, I thought I felt it look at me. Then it went back to work.

"The slot!" I shouted. "Books go in the slot at the desk! You've got about ninety seconds from when it stamps you!"

That changed things. People stopped running away from the Librarian and started running *toward* the desk. For about two minutes, it almost worked.

Then the Librarian started stamping faster.

---

**[Owen Park — Due: 00:00:58]**
**[Rugby Shirt (Jonah Webb) — Due: 00:01:12]**
**[Maya Reyes — Due: 00:01:25]**
**[Eli Marsh — Due: 00:01:29]**

I didn't even feel it get me. One second my hand was empty. The next second I was holding a book called *A Short History of Levees,* and it wouldn't come off.

It was stamping someone every five or six seconds now. People were returning books and getting stamped again before they'd taken three steps from the desk. Nobody was hitting it anymore. Nobody had time.

It was the old life in miniature. You couldn't win a fight by surviving it. Sooner or later, someone had to stand still and let the thing hit them, so everybody else could work.

Nora was standing in the middle of the room with her axe, looking at me.

She'd been watching the whole time. She'd been waiting.

"Don't," I said.

"You said I'm the only one who can take a hit."

"The note said—"

"The note isn't here." She turned to face the Librarian. "You are. So tell me what to do."

I looked at the room. At Owen, with fifty seconds on his book, pushing one of the Artisans toward the desk. At the Librarian, lifting its stamp again. At the shelves around us, tall and dark, and at the Librarian's back, where the paper robe hung down, and where something under the pages was a slightly different color. Brown, like old leather.

**[Spine — 400 Merit]**

A book's spine. A thing with a spine can be broken.

"Thirty seconds," I said. "Hold the Line for thirty seconds. Then drop it. Whatever it gives you, you run to the desk. I'll read your timers out loud. If I say *go*, you go, even if I'm in the middle of something."

"And what are you doing?"

"Something stupid," I said, and ran for the shelves.

---

**[Nora Marsh used Hold the Line.]**

The Librarian stopped walking.

It turned, slowly, toward my sister, and every other person in the room stopped mattering to it. It crossed the reading room in four long strides and pressed the stamp to her shoulder.

*Thunk.*

A book appeared in her left arm. She held the axe in her right. The Librarian stamped her again.

*Thunk.*

A second book.

"Nora, one-twenty and one-fifteen!" I yelled. I was already climbing. The shelf nearest to the circulation desk was maybe four meters high, dark oak, heavy and old. Books slid out under my feet as I scrambled up it, and I grabbed the upper shelves with my free hand, and the book about levees banged against my ribs with every pull. "Everyone else, return your books! It's not looking at you!"

*Thunk.*

"One-oh-eight!"

I reached the top of the shelf and crouched there, breathing hard. Below me, the Librarian stood over my sister, stamping her again and again with the calm of someone processing paperwork, and Nora stood there and took it with her jaw clenched, arms full of books.

*Thunk.* *Thunk.*

**[Nora Marsh — 5 items checked out. Earliest due: 00:00:51]**

"Nora, *drop it now!*"

She dropped Hold the Line and ran for the desk.

The Librarian turned to follow her, and in turning it put its back to my shelf, and the brown leather spine between its shoulders was a meter and a half below me and a meter and a half out.

I had a letter opener. I'd carried it the whole way from the Market District. It was the only weapon I'd ever used on anything that fought back.

I jumped.

It isn't a long drop. It felt like one. I landed on the Librarian's back with both knees, and it was soft and wet and cold, like landing on a pile of soaked newspaper, and I grabbed a fistful of pages to hold myself on and drove the letter opener down into the spine with everything I had.

It went in about two centimeters and stuck.

The Librarian made a noise. It wasn't a scream. It was worse. It was the sound of a hundred pages tearing at once, and it reached back for me with one long arm.

**[Eli Marsh — Due: 00:00:19]**

"*Jonah!*" I screamed.

The rugby shirt had been the strongest of us since the first leech. He'd just returned his book. He turned, saw me, saw where the letter opener was stuck, and understood. He ran at the Librarian with his sword up and put his whole body behind one swing, and it didn't hit the Librarian. It hit the handle of my letter opener, like a hammer on a nail.

The spine cracked.

It sounded exactly like breaking the spine of a new paperback. A dry, satisfying snap.

Every page on the Librarian's body let go at once.

I fell through it. It was like falling through a wet paper bag. I hit the marble on my back with a thousand soaked pages coming down on top of me like snow, and the air went out of my lungs, and the book about levees was gone from my hands, and above me the green reading lamps flickered and brightened and turned, one by one, to an ordinary warm yellow.

**[The Drowned Librarian has been slain.]**

I lay there. Somewhere in the paper, someone was laughing. It sounded like Nora. It also sounded like crying.

**[Nora Marsh — All items returned.]**

Across the whole city, in every sealed zone, every survivor was looking up at a notice with my name on it. The name of an Appraiser. In the old life, Zone Nine had never been cleared. It had just drained, three days in, when the tutorial ended, and they'd found eleven people alive.

I opened the map.

**[Zone Nine — Survivors: 1,041]**

It had stopped counting down.

I lay on the floor of the library in a drift of wet paper and stared at that number until it blurred, and I didn't try to stop it blurring. Then Nora's face appeared above me, upside down, her hair full of paper scraps, and she held out a hand.

"You jumped on it," she said. "You *jumped on it*. You. Mr. Doesn't-Make-You-Fight."

"Jonah did the hard part."

"I heard that," said Jonah, from somewhere.

She pulled me up. I stood in the middle of the reading room, soaked, with a letter opener that was now bent at a forty-five-degree angle, and looked out through the tall arched windows. The black water was sliding away. You could watch it go. The lawn was coming back, mud and dead grass and the old oak trees, and the boats were sitting on the steps, high and dry, and the green sky was cracking open in the east, showing a strip of plain, ordinary blue.

And down by Harlan Hall, where the dorm's basement had been, the lake had drained off a long pale body lying in the mud. The Hollow Matron. Without the water, it had simply stopped. It had dragged its prizes home before the end. Next to it, in the mud, lay a pillowcase tied shut with a phone charger cable, and a small gold glint that could only be the compass.

"Owen," I said, "get your clipboard. We're going to get everybody's money back."

---

The Librarian had dropped three things.

The stamp, which Jonah picked up and, to general horror, tried on his own hand before anyone could stop him. It didn't do anything. **[Librarian's Date Stamp — True Value: 90 Merit.]** The spine, cracked in half, which I took. **[Cracked Spine — True Value: 400 Merit.]** And a single sheet of paper, lying face down on the circulation desk exactly where the Librarian had been standing when we walked in.

It wasn't wet. Everything else in the room was soaked through, and this one sheet was bone dry, and the color of old paper. The same color as my loan agreement.

I turned it over.

It was a page from a ledger. A real one, with columns ruled in faded red ink. The same hand-lettered writing I'd seen in the window on the hundredth floor. At the top it said *Accounts — Borrower: Marsh, E.* and underneath that, there were seven lines.

**[Loan #1 — Regression. Status: Defaulted. Collateral seized.]**
**[Loan #2 — Regression. Status: Defaulted. Collateral seized.]**
**[Loan #3 — Regression. Status: Defaulted. Collateral seized.]**
**[Loan #4 — Regression. Status: Defaulted. Collateral seized.]**
**[Loan #5 — Regression. Status: Defaulted. Collateral seized.]**
**[Loan #6 — Regression. Collateral: Nora Marsh. Status: Defaulted. Collection pending.]**
**[Loan #7 — Regression. Collateral: Nora Marsh. Status: Active.]**

Seven.

I wasn't the man who'd finally found a way to beat the Exchange. I was the seventh one to try.
