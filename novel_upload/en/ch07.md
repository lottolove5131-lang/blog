# Chapter 7: Market Open

At midnight on Day Four, the Exchange opened for business, and the whole city went to see it.

It happened in the square in front of the old courthouse, the same steps where Damon had given his speech on Day Zero. At 00:00, a column of gold light came down out of the night sky and hit the paving stones, and when it faded there was a board standing in the middle of the square. Ten meters tall. Twenty wide. Made of something like glass, filled from edge to edge with scrolling gold text.

**[PUBLIC EXCHANGE — REGIONAL BOARD]**
**[Listings: 14,208]**
**[Fee: 5% on all completed sales.]**

People had been camping in the square since sunset. By the time the board lit up, there were thousands of them, holding their tutorial loot in grocery bags and backpacks and, in one case, a wheelbarrow. As soon as the board went live, they surged toward it, and within a minute the listings started climbing so fast that the numbers blurred.

**[Listings: 19,440]**
**[Listings: 26,091]**

Everybody was selling. Nobody knew what anything was worth.

I stood at the back of the crowd, next to Nora, with thirteen Merit in my account, and looked at it the way a starving man looks at a buffet.

"You're smiling," Nora said. "It's creepy."

"This is the only night it'll ever look like this," I said. "In a week, everyone will know the prices. In a month, the guilds will control them. Right now, it's just fourteen thousand people with no idea what they're holding."

"And you know."

"I know two numbers," I said. "They know one."

---

I'd been awake half the night making a list, and most of it was useless.

The old life had given me seven years of prices. The problem was that most of them only mattered later. I knew that Tallyhound left eyes would be worth fifty Merit apiece in Year Two, once Artisans figured out how to turn them into lenses. That didn't help me. I didn't have two years. I had ten days and two thousand Merit to find.

So I'd gone through the list looking for one thing: something cheap tonight that would be expensive by Day Ten.

I found it on the third page of listings.

**[Emberleaf Salve (x12) — Listed at: 3 Merit each]**

**[Appraise]**

**[Emberleaf Salve — Market Value: 3 Merit | True Value: 34 Merit]**
**[Effect: Reduces fire damage taken by 40% for one hour.]**

In the old life, the Spire's first floor opened on Day Six. The Exchange called it *The Kiln.* It was a long stone corridor that heated up every ten minutes until the walls glowed, and the first three raid parties that went in didn't come out. By the evening of Day Six, anyone who could spell *fire resistance* was paying forty Merit a jar for Emberleaf Salve, and by Day Eight it was sixty, and the Artisans who'd been selling it for three Merit on Day Four were throwing up in alleys when they saw the board.

Right now, it was a green paste that Artisans made out of a weed, and nobody wanted it.

"How much can we buy?" Nora asked when I explained.

"With thirteen Merit? Four jars."

"And if we had more?"

I looked at her. She was already pulling up her own status.

"I've got two hundred and seventy," she said. "From the tutorial. My share of the clear bonus, and the cores."

"No."

"I didn't ask."

"Nora, it's a bet. If I'm wrong—"

"Are you wrong?"

"I don't think so. But—"

"Then it's not a bet. It's a loan." She crossed her arms. "You still owe me forty dollars from Christmas. I'm adding two hundred and fifty Merit to it. You can pay me back when the salve does whatever you think it's going to do."

"Two-fifty. Plus forty dollars."

"Plus interest," said my sister, who had apparently been listening carefully when I told her about the Lender. "One and a half percent a day. That seems to be the going rate."

---

We bought every jar of Emberleaf Salve on the board.

It took an hour. Most of it was listed in lots of five or ten by Artisans who'd crafted it during the tutorial out of boredom, and every time we bought a lot, another one showed up. A few sellers relisted at four Merit when they saw the demand. One tried seven. I let that one sit.

Around the fortieth jar, an Artisan found us. He was a skinny man in a hardware-store apron with green stains up to his elbows, and he'd noticed that somebody was buying every jar he listed within about ten seconds.

"You're the one cleaning me out," he said. "What do you want with all of it?"

"I have a rash," I said.

He looked at me, then at Nora, then back at me. "Eighty jars' worth?"

"It's a very bad rash."

He went away muttering. Five minutes later, a new lot of his showed up on the board, at four Merit instead of three. I bought it anyway. He'd earned the extra Merit for asking.

**[Emberleaf Salve purchased (x86). Total cost: 261 Merit.]**
**[Balance (Eli): 2 Merit]**

Eighty-six jars. At thirty-four Merit each, it was almost three thousand Merit in true value, stacked in a hotel room we'd broken into on Fifth Street because it had a door that locked. If the Kiln opened on Day Six the way it had last time, I'd clear my installment with room to spare.

If it didn't, I'd just spent my sister's whole tutorial on green paste.

I lay awake that night on the hotel room's second bed and listened to Nora breathe, and did the math again, and again, until I fell asleep in the middle of it.

---

On the afternoon of Day Four, Damon Kreel founded the Vanguard Guild.

I watched it happen on the board.

**[REGIONAL NOTICE]**
**[A new Guild has been chartered: VANGUARD.]**
**[Guild Master: Damon Kreel (Warrior, Lv. 11)]**
**[Members: 31]**

It had taken him fifteen days to found Vanguard last time. This time, it had taken four. I wondered, not for the first time, how much of the old timeline had already shifted because of what I'd done on Day Zero. Maybe the laugh on the courthouse steps had lit a fire under him. Maybe he'd just learned faster, without an Appraiser to lean on.

Level eleven. He'd spent the tutorial the way he always did, at the front of a crowd, taking the kills.

A guild charter cost a thousand Merit. Nobody in the region had a thousand Merit on Day Four, not even Damon. I pulled up the listing details, curious, and found the answer at the bottom.

**[Charter funded by: Exchange Charter Credit (3,000 Merit). Terms: 6% weekly.]**

He'd borrowed it. Three thousand Merit from the Exchange itself, at a rate that made my Lender look like a charity. A thousand for the charter. Two thousand for something else.

I found out what at about six o'clock that evening, when he walked up to us in the square.

---

Nora and I were standing near the board, watching the Emberleaf listings. They hadn't moved. I checked them every ten minutes, which Nora said made me look like a man waiting for a lottery draw. Around us, the crowd had thinned out into something more like a market: people wandering between the board and the little stalls that had popped up along the edge of the square, selling food, selling water, selling their services as guards and porters and, in one very optimistic case, Appraisers.

Then Nora said, "Eli," in a low voice, and I turned around.

Damon had brought six people with him. All of them were Warriors, all of them big, and all of them wore the same gray armband with a white V on it. He'd found time to make armbands. That was very Damon.

He didn't look at me. He looked at Nora.

"Nora Marsh," he said, with the smile. "The Warden of Zone Nine. I've been hoping to meet you."

"Hi," said Nora.

"I'll keep this short, because I know everybody in the city wants a minute of your time right now." He held out his hand, and she didn't take it, and he let it drop without any sign that he'd noticed. "You're the only Warden in the region who cleared a zone. You're the only Warden in the region who's level ten. And you're the only person I've heard of who stood in front of a zone boss on purpose. Vanguard's going into the Spire the day it opens. I want you at the front."

"No," I said.

He glanced at me for the first time. "I'm sorry. I was talking to your sister."

"She's not interested."

"I think she can tell me that herself." He turned back to her. "One year, exclusive. We cover your gear. We cover your healing. We split loot evenly across the guild, which I'm told is something you care about." That was a dig at me, and it landed. He'd heard about Owen's clipboard. "And there's a signing bonus."

"How much?" said Nora.

"Two thousand Merit."

I felt the number land in my stomach like a stone.

Two thousand. My second installment, to the Merit. He didn't know that. He couldn't. It was a round number, a big number, the kind of number that makes people stop thinking. It was a coincidence.

Nora didn't look at me. She didn't have to.

"I'll think about it," she said.

"Take your time." Damon smiled at her again. "But not too much. The Spire opens in two days, and the front line fills up fast."

He left. His six Warriors went with him. I watched the gray armbands disappear into the crowd and didn't say anything until they were gone.

"Nora."

"I said I'll think about it."

"He left me on the hundredth floor to die."

"You told me." She was looking at the board. At the Emberleaf listings, which still said three Merit. "In another life. He hasn't done it yet in this one."

"That's not how it works."

"Isn't it? You laughed at him in front of a crowd, and he founded his guild eleven days early. Things change, Eli. That's the whole point of you." She finally looked at me. "Is the salve going to work?"

"Yes."

"You don't know that."

"I—" I stopped. She was right. I didn't. I knew what had happened last time. That was all I'd ever known.

---

On the morning of Day Five, we walked downtown to look at the Spire.

Half the city had the same idea. The tower stood where the old transit center used to be, a white needle so tall that the top of it disappeared into a thin haze, and the plaza around its base was crowded with tents and cooking fires and people just sitting on the ground, staring up. The door at the bottom was a plain arch of white stone, three meters high, filled with something like frosted glass. A countdown hung above it.

**[Spire, Floor 1 — Opens in: 20:14:33]**

"It's so *big*," Nora said.

"It gets bigger," I said. "Inside."

She looked at me. "Were you ever in there? The first floor?"

"Once. Week Three." I kept my eyes on the door. "I didn't go in, exactly. I stood at the entrance with a cart of water, and when the raid parties came back out, I priced what they were carrying. And what the ones who didn't walk out had been carrying. The guild wanted to know what the gear was worth before they decided whether to go back for it."

Nora didn't say anything for a while.

"The corridor has alcoves," I said, because it was easier to talk about the building than about the cart. "Every thirty meters, on the left side. The heat doesn't reach the back of them. Nobody figured that out until Week Two. By then, about four hundred people had died in there."

"Are you going to tell anyone?"

"I'm going to sell it," I said. "Once I've paid the Lender. Information is the only thing I have that nobody else has."

"That's a horrible thing to say."

"It's a horrible world. I didn't price it." I looked at her. "But yes. I'll tell people. I'll make sure Owen and Jonah know before they go anywhere near it."

She nodded slowly. Then she pulled her hoodie sleeves down over her hands, the way she did when she was cold or thinking.

"The page said *collection pending*," she said. "For number six. The one with my name."

"Yes."

"So someone's coming. For me."

"Maybe."

"Then we'll be ready," she said, with a lot more confidence than I felt. "You know stuff. I can take a hit. That's a pretty good team."

We walked back through the plaza. On the way, I checked the board. Emberleaf Salve: three Merit.

---

On the evening of Day Five, Nora didn't come back to the hotel.

I waited until nine. Then I went looking. The square, the stalls, the streets around the courthouse. I asked strangers if they'd seen a Warden, tall, dark hair tied back badly. One of them had. She'd gone into the old Merchant Row Hotel an hour ago with a man in a gray armband.

Vanguard's new guild hall.

I found her on the front steps, coming out. She stopped when she saw me. She had a piece of paper in her hand, folded in thirds, and she didn't try to hide it.

"Before you say anything," she said, "I did the math."

"Nora."

"I *did the math*, Eli. You need the salve to go up more than ten times in three days. If the Spire opens late, or if the Kiln isn't what you remember, or if some Artisan figures out how to make it faster and floods the board, you've got two Merit and a hotel room full of paste. And then what? Then he takes whatever it is you won't tell me about." Her voice cracked on the last part. She got it back. "I'm not going to watch you sell pieces of yourself on a roof again. You don't get to do that alone anymore."

She held out the paper.

"Signing bonus is two thousand," she said. "It's yours."

I took the contract. My hands were steady. I don't know how.

**[Appraise]**

**[Vanguard Guild — Member Agreement (Nora Marsh) — EXECUTED]**
**[Term: One (1) year. Exclusive.]**
**[Signing Bonus: 2,000 Merit]**
**[Hidden Clause (1): Signing Bonus payable upon the Guild's first Spire floor clear.]**
**[Hidden Clause (2): Guild Master may designate any member as Line Holder for any encounter. Refusal constitutes breach.]**
**[Penalty for breach: Forfeiture of all Merit and equipment, plus 5,000 Merit.]**

I read it the way I'd read the loan on the hundredth floor. Twice, slowly, every line.

She hadn't been paid anything. She wouldn't be, until Vanguard cleared a floor. And the only way Vanguard cleared the Kiln on Day Six was if someone stood at the front of that burning corridor, holding the line, while everyone else ran past.

*You were always the cheapest thing in the room, Eli.*

He hadn't changed at all. He'd just found a better price tag.

This one could take a hit.
