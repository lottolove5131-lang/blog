# Chapter 28: Senior Claim

**[Senior Lien — Door opens in: 00:00:03]**

The wood under my palm was warm now, warm like a mug somebody had just put down. The two marks at the bottom of the carving burned red from the inside, the dark red of ruled lines on an old ledger page.

*— E.*

Two quick knocks had come through the door. Then the pause.

I knew that pause. I'd lain awake in my room at eleven years old waiting for the end of it, so I'd know it was her and not a monster. Two quick, a pause, one slow. Nora's knock. *When I'm ready, I'll knock,* he'd said to me in the yellow margin. *It was hers first. I borrowed it.*

**[00:00:02]**

Behind me, the steps of Hale Memorial Library had gone quiet. Not peaceful. Quiet the way the Kiln went quiet in the half second before discharge.

Through the dusty arched window beside the door, I could see Vesper Kaine at the circulation desk, one finger resting on the open file as if she were keeping her place in a hymnal. *Loan #6 — Marsh, N.*

**[00:00:01]**

Nora came off her end of the landing and stood at my shoulder, her shield still on her arm. Her hand closed on the back of my coat, just holding on, the way she'd held on in the back seat of the car when she was little and the road got bumpy.

"Eli."

"I know."

**[12:00:00]**

One slow knock.

It went through the wood and into my hand and up my arm, and two windows opened at once.

The first came from inside the library. It was a color I'd seen on twenty-two wrists for three weeks: the flat gray of cold ash, with letters stamped into it like a rubber seal.

**[ASHGRAVE RECOVERIES — COLLECTION ORDER]**
**[Account: Loan #6 (Purchased). Original Borrower: Marsh, E. (Loop 6)]**
**[Collateral: Nora Marsh]**
**[Status: Executing.]**

The second window was the color of old paper, and the ink on it was darker than any window the Lender had ever shown me, brown-black, soaked deep into the page. I'd seen it once before, on the fourth floor of Harlan Hall, at two in the morning, with my sister asleep on my shoulder.

**[LIEN — SENIOR]**
**[Lienholder: E█i M█rsh (Bearer Form)]**
**[Recorded: Year 9, Day 3]**
**[Collateral: Nora Marsh (Loop 7)]**
**[Window of Exercise: OPEN]**

Down the steps, someone gasped. One of the twenty-two.

I looked back. The gray-corded collectors were still standing where they'd stopped, in a loose crowd at the foot of the stairs, blind without their field agent in sight. Damon was face down in the mud below them with Silas Rook's knee in the middle of his back. Now every cord on every wrist was glowing, faint ash-colored light, running up their arms like a signal.

And on my sister's right wrist, where nothing had been a second ago, a gray thread was drawing itself around the bone. One loop. Then the start of a second.

Nora looked down at it.

"Eli," she said again, very calmly, and that was worse than if she'd screamed.

I faced the door. I'd practiced the words for six days, out loud, in the hotel bathroom with the fan on, because Owen had told me that contracts liked exact language and I'd told him I knew that and then practiced anyway.

"As holder of the senior lien," I said, "I exercise it. I foreclose on the collateral."

The paper window turned over like a page.

**[Bearer verification.]**
**[Presented by: Eli Marsh (Loop 7).]**
**[Name: E█i M█rsh — MATCH.]**
**[Private Account: ACTIVE (Loan #7).]**
**[Present at the Door: CONFIRMED.]**
**[Claim RECOGNIZED.]**

Then the ledger did what ledgers do. It went down the list.

**[Foreclosure in progress. Junior claims against the collateral will be extinguished.]**
**[Loan #6 (Holder: Ashgrave Recoveries) — Junior. EXTINGUISHED.]**

Through the window, Vesper's file went white.

I watched it happen. The typed lines on the first page faded from the top down, like a receipt left in a hot car, and then the second page, and then all of them at once. She lifted her finger off the blank paper and looked at it. Then she closed the folder, stood up, walked three steps to the RETURNS slot, and pushed it through.

**[Loan #7 — Collateral Pledge (Nora Marsh) — Junior. EXTINGUISHED.]**

On Nora's wrist, the gray thread stopped halfway through its second loop. It hung there for a moment. Then it came apart into ash and blew off her skin like the end of a cigarette.

Down the steps, the cords on the twenty-two didn't come off. Their light just went out. They were somebody else's paper. That was a problem for later.

In the mud, Damon turned his head sideways to look at the gray cord on his own wrist, the one he'd tied himself. It had gone slack. He didn't try to take it off.

Silas still had a knee in his back and a dueling sword resting flat across his shoulders. He'd spent the last five minutes telling Damon, in a low, even voice, exactly what the end of an Ashgrave contract looked like from the inside. Now he'd stopped. He was looking up past me, at the door.

"That's it," he said, not to anyone. "That's the window. I never saw it from this side."

Bram stood alone at his end of the landing with his shield still up, though nobody was pushing on it. He was watching one of the collectors at the foot of the stairs, a short woman with a burn scar on her chin, as if she might look up at him and know who he was.

She didn't. Not yet.

Owen was at the bottom of the stairs with his clipboard pressed against his chest. I saw him write something. Later he showed me the line. *12:00:00 — Loan 6 extinguished. Witnessed.* He'd underlined *witnessed* twice.

---

The doors opened.

Nobody touched them. They swung inward, slowly, both at once, and the carving on the waterline stain split down the middle where the two halves parted. Cold came out, the damp cold of a basement full of paper.

Someone was standing in the doorframe.

He was my height. He stood the way I stood when I'd been on my feet too long, weight on the left leg. He wore a coat that had been mine in Year Five, the long brown one with the reinforced pockets that I'd bought off a dead Ranger's estate for nine Merit and wore until it fell apart. His hair had gray in it at the temples. His left hand, from the fingertips up past the wrist, was stained dark, the way your hand gets when a pen leaks in your pocket and you don't notice for an hour. Except it went all the way up under his sleeve.

Thirty-four years old. I knew that the way I knew my own price.

I appraised him anyway. I couldn't help it.

**[Eli Marsh (Loop 6) — Appraiser (Lv. 58)]**
**[Status: Lien of record. Pending discharge.]**

He looked at me first. Just a glance, quick, the way you check a total you already know is right.

Then he looked past me.

I heard Nora stop breathing.

"You can see him," I said.

"Yes." It came out of her very small.

The man in the doorway smiled. It was my smile. I'd never seen it from the outside, and I didn't like how tired it was.

"Hi, Nora," he said.

She didn't answer. Her hand was still on my coat. I could feel it shaking through the fabric.

"I don't have long," he said. "I'm what's left of a line in a book, and the book's being closed. So I'll just say the one thing." He looked at her, and his face did something I recognized from the inside, the thing it did when I was about to do math I didn't want the answer to. "Don't hold the line. Not for him. Not for anybody. Let somebody stand next to you."

Nora let go of my coat.

She stepped past me, up to the threshold, until she was close enough to touch him, and she didn't. She stood there in front of a version of her brother who had watched her die on the eighty-eighth floor, and she looked at his stained hand, and the gray in his hair, and the coat.

"You wrote it on a door," she said. "You had nine years, and you wrote it on a *door*."

"I didn't have a phone."

"You're so stupid."

It was the same voice she'd used on the library steps on Day Three, and on the hotel floor, and at the bottom of the courthouse steps, and in a hundred other places I'd only half heard because I was busy doing arithmetic. Furious and wet. The voice she used when she was too relieved to be polite about it.

He laughed. It cracked in the middle, the way mine did.

"Yeah," he said. "I know. It runs in the family."

Then he looked at me again.

**[Lien Release — Requires Signature of Exercising Holder.]**
**[Effect: Lien discharged. Collateral released from all ledgers. Lienholder of record (Loop 6) will be removed.]**
**[Sign? Y / N]**

Removed. The ledger still liked small, boring words.

"You can say no," he said to me. "You'd keep the lien. You'd keep her in a book. Mine, but still a book."

"You wanted this."

"I asked for it. Year Nine, Day Three, in a room you've never seen. It's not the same thing as wanting it." He put his inked hand in his coat pocket, the way I did when I didn't want people to see it shake. "Go on. You've got another window coming, and it's worse than this one."

"How do you know?"

"Because I'm the one who didn't have a buyer," he said. "Remember what I told you."

*There has to be a buyer standing there.* He'd said it in the yellow margin, in the place where the red lines ran to the horizon. I'd said I would. I still didn't know how.

I pressed my thumb to the window.

**[LIEN DISCHARGED.]**

He came apart like paper.

Not burning, not tearing. More like the pages of a book that's been underwater, when you lift it and the binding finally lets go, and everything slides apart in thin gray sheets. The coat went first, then his hands, then his face, still smiling, and the sheets drifted down onto the threshold, and the threshold was dry stone, and when they touched it they were gone.

The carving on the door was gone too. Just the old waterline. Just the stain.

Nora stood on the top step and stared at the empty doorframe. Her shoulders were moving. She didn't make any noise.

I appraised her, because I'm a coward and it was easier than looking at her face.

**[Appraise]**

**[Nora Marsh — Human — Warden (Lv. 18)]**
**[Market Value: — Not for sale.]**
**[True Value: — Not for sale.]**
**[Liens: NONE.]**

I read it four times. *None.* For thirty days that line had been the first thing I checked in the morning and the last thing I checked at night, and every time there'd been a name on it, and now there wasn't. She belonged to nobody's ledger.

"Eli," said Jonah, from somewhere below me. "Is that it? Did it work?"

I opened my mouth to answer him.

The air next to my left shoulder went dark.

---

I heard the chair before I saw it. Four wooden legs scraping across the stone landing, unhurried, the way someone drags a chair to a table where they mean to stay for a while. Then the place where the light didn't reach had a shape, and the shape sat down.

Long coat. Narrow shoulders. The smooth blank oval where a face should be. The ledger on his knee, thick as a brick.

"Mr. Marsh," said the Lender. "Congratulations. That was very cleanly done. Your predecessor would be proud, if he were anywhere."

Nora didn't turn. She couldn't see him. But Vesper, standing in the library doorway now with her hands in her coat pockets, looked straight at the chair, and her mouth went thin. Of course she could see him. She'd had an account once.

"You've come to collect," I said.

"I've come because the paperwork requires it." He opened the ledger. "You'll recall the original terms. You negotiated them yourself, on the hundredth floor. The collateral was changed from your existence to your most valuable possession. Your sister. A very good trade. I admired it."

"And now she's not pledged to anyone."

"Exactly. Which leaves Loan Seven without collateral. And an agreement can't sit on nothing, Mr. Marsh. That's not a rule of mine. That's arithmetic." He turned the page toward me, just slightly, the way he'd done in the first life. "So it reverts."

**[LOAN #7 — COLLATERAL EXTINGUISHED.]**
**[Per original terms (Clause 1, as drafted): Collateral substitution — Borrower's Existence.]**
**[Outstanding Principal: 11,562 Merit.]**

I did the math anyway. I always do. Ten thousand four hundred and eighteen on Day Twenty-Three. Seven days at one and a half percent, compounding. Eleven thousand five hundred and sixty-two.

He hadn't cheated me by a single Merit. He never did. That was the frightening thing about him.

"It's an unsecured loan now, Mr. Marsh," the Lender said, almost apologetically. "Well. Secured by you, which in my business amounts to the same. I'm afraid I have no choice but to call it."

"You always have a choice."

"I have a fiduciary duty," he said. "To myself."

He uncapped his pen.

The window that opened in front of me was the old paper color, but the border was red. I'd never seen a red border on anything of his.

**[LOAN #7 — CALLED IN FULL.]**
**[Amount Due: 11,562 Merit.]**
**[Time to Pay: 00:59:59]**
**[Current Balance: 419 Merit.]**
**[Upon Default: Collateral (Borrower's Existence) seized in full and RETROACTIVELY.]**

Retroactively.

I'd read that word with a slab of stone on my legs, and I'd haggled it off the table, and here it was again, as if it had been sitting in the next room the whole time waiting for me to finish.

If I'd never existed, I'd never come back. Nobody would have knocked on the fourth-floor lounge door in Harlan Hall. Nobody would have thrown a pillowcase into a lake. Zone Nine would have drained on the morning of Day Three, the way it had the first time, and they'd have found eleven people alive.

And the girl standing on the top step, with ash on her wrist and her brother's ghost still drifting off the threshold in front of her, would have her name on a list again.

**[00:59:51]**

Four hundred and nineteen Merit. Eleven thousand five hundred and sixty-two owed.

I said it out loud, the way I always do, so somebody besides me would know the number.

"Eleven thousand one hundred and forty-three short."
