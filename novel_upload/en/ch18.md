# Chapter 18: The Counting House

We went in at seven in the morning on Day Eighteen with forty meters of rope, five days, and a ledger that said three thousand five hundred at the top of a fresh page.

Under it, in my handwriting:

*795. House money in Owen's column. Short: everything else.*

Under that, in Nora's:

*Hold the Line: 0 uses.*

The black door at the foot of the Spire had a new staircase behind it now, wide and white, leading up past the Kiln's corridor through a seam in the stone that hadn't been there a week ago. It was warm at the bottom and cold at the top. At the top there was a pair of bronze doors, and on the doors, cast into the metal in raised letters like the lid of an old cash box:

**[Spire, Floor 2: The Counting House]**
**[Floor Boss: The Teller. Condition of Clear: Account settled.]**

"Settled," Owen said, reading over my shoulder. "Not slain."

"It's a counting house," I said. "Everything in here is going to want paying."

---

The hall behind the doors was the biggest room I'd ever stood in, and I'd stood on the hundredth floor.

White marble. Long, so long the far end was gray with distance. Columns every twenty meters, each one thick as a car, and between them, down the center of the hall, hung a set of scales. A real set. The beam ran nearly the full width of the room, high up near the ceiling, and from each end of it chains as thick as my arm dropped to two great brass pans that rested a meter above the floor. The left pan held a heap of gold coins. The right one held nothing at all. The beam was perfectly level anyway, which my brain refused to accept every time I looked at it.

Things moved in the aisles between the columns. They were the size of a man, built out of gold coins that clinked and shifted when they walked, and where their faces should have been was a single coin, larger than the others, stamped with a profile I didn't recognize.

**[Gold-Coin Golem, Lv. 14]**
**[Market Value: 60 Merit | True Value: 190 Merit]**
**[Note: Struck coins scatter and re-form. Disperse the face-coin to stop re-forming.]**

"Hit the face," I said. "The face-coin's the only one that matters. Everything else just falls down and gets up again."

"Everything always falls down and gets up again," Jonah muttered, and nocked an arrow. He'd stopped arguing about the bow ten days ago. The sword was for leeches. The bow was for faces.

The first golem saw us at the third column and came at Bram in a long clattering run. Bram took it on his shield. Coins sprayed across the marble like a dropped till. Nora stepped up on his left side, not ahead of him, beside him, her axe held low, and Owen's riot shield came up on Bram's right.

They'd been practicing it every evening since Day Thirteen behind the House. Two shields, one axe. Nobody alone at the front.

The golem's coins swirled and started to pull back together. Jonah shot the face-coin out of the middle of the swirl. The whole thing collapsed into a pile of loose gold, and the gold went dull and flat and turned into ordinary bronze discs that the Exchange valued at nothing.

"That's mean," Nora said, nudging the pile with her boot. "It looks like money and then it isn't."

"Most things in here will," I said.

---

The windows were along the left-hand wall.

There were nine of them, tall arched openings in the marble with brass grilles and a polished counter in front of each, like an old bank. Above every window was a number in gold. Behind every grille there was a hand. Just a hand, pale, in a starched cuff, resting on the counter, and every so often it moved, and stamped something, and went still again.

When I got within ten meters of Window One, a notice opened in front of me in the Exchange's gold.

**[Teller Window 1 — Floor Exchange]**
**[Buys: Gold-Coin Golem face-coin @ 52. Sells: @ 61.]**

"It's a shop," Owen said.

"It's nine shops," I said. I was already walking. "And they don't talk to each other."

I went down the wall slowly, reading. The others came with me in formation, Bram's shield turning toward every clink in the aisles. Every window traded the same handful of things: face-coins, golem cores, a flat gray plate the golems carried inside their chests like a breastbone. And every window's numbers were different. Not a little different. Window Three was selling golem cores at forty. Window Seven was buying them at seventy.

I stood in front of Window Seven and did the math three times, out loud, because I didn't trust it.

"Buy at forty on Three. Walk two hundred meters. Sell at seventy on Seven. Thirty a core."

"That can't be right," said Owen.

**[Appraise]**

I pointed it at the window instead of the item.

**[Teller Window 7 — Price set by local balance (Pan 7). Pan 7 is underweight in Cores.]**
**[Note: Each window's prices follow its own pan. Pans rebalance every 6 hours.]**

"Each window has its own little scale somewhere," I said. "Window Seven's scale is short on cores, so it pays more. Window Three's got too many, so it's dumping them. They'll fix themselves every six hours." I looked down at the counter. "Until then, it's free money."

"There's no such thing as free money," Nora said. "You told me that. On the roof."

"There's no such thing as free money for long," I said. "Six hours is long enough."

---

We stood in a circle by the fourth column, while Bram faced the aisle, and I put it to them the way Owen would want it put. Numbers on the table.

"It's my money going in. Seven hundred and ninety-five is everything I've got. If Window Three closes on us or the golems get between us and Seven, I lose it, and we're all three thousand five hundred short instead of two thousand seven hundred and five." I looked around the circle. "If it works, it's six hundred. Two rounds. I'd like to keep it for the installment. Not split it. I'm asking."

Nobody spoke for a second.

"It's your capital," Owen said finally. "We'd be carrying it across the hall for you. That's a service. I'd charge two percent for that."

"Owen."

"I'm *joking.*" He was already writing on the clipboard. "Partially."

"Equal fifths is for what we kill," Bram said. "This isn't killing. It's shopping. You shop, Eli. We'll stand next to you while you do it."

Jonah shrugged. "I don't want to carry coins. They're loud."

Nora was the last. She took the ledger out of my coat pocket without asking, which she'd been doing since Day Thirteen, and wrote under my line: *Window arbitrage: party agrees, profit to Eli → installment. Witnessed.* Then she held out the pen to Owen, who signed it, and Owen held it out to Jonah, who signed it, and Bram signed it last in big blocky letters that took up two lines.

"Now it's a contract," Nora said. "Go shop."

---

Window Three let me buy ten cores at a time. The hand behind the grille counted them out onto the counter one by one, and stamped my receipt, and went still.

**[Purchased: Gold-Coin Golem Core ×10 @ 40. Total: 400 Merit.]**
**[Balance: 395 Merit]**

Three hundred and ninety-five Merit in my account in the middle of a monster floor. I didn't like how it felt. Nora walked on my left, Bram on my right, Jonah behind, Owen ahead with the shield. Two hundred meters. Two golems came at us on the way, out of the space between columns six and seven; Bram blocked one, Nora's axe took the face off the other, and Jonah got the first one's face-coin with his second arrow.

Window Seven took my ten cores and paid seventy each, with no fee. The floor windows didn't charge the Exchange's five percent. That had surprised me more than anything else so far.

**[Sold: Gold-Coin Golem Core ×10 @ 70. Total: 700 Merit.]**
**[Balance: 1,095 Merit]**

"Three hundred," I said.

Window Seven's buy price dropped to sixty-two the moment I sold. The pan was full. We'd have to wait for it to empty.

So we waited. We cleared the aisles around the windows for six hours, and the party split the golem salvage equally, the way it always did, and Owen wrote it all down. At the sixth hour exactly, a long shiver ran down the beam of the great scale overhead and the chains rang like bells, and when I checked the windows, Three was selling at forty again and Seven was buying at seventy.

Second round. Four hundred out. Seven hundred in.

**[Balance: 1,395 Merit]**

"Seven ninety-five plus three hundred plus three hundred," I said. "One thousand three hundred ninety-five. Six hundred clean."

"Witnessed," said Nora, and wrote it down.

By the third rebalance there were other parties in the hall, and one of them had a Zone Two Appraiser with them, a thin kid in a borrowed coat, and I watched him stand in front of Window Three with his mouth open, and then run to Window Seven. The gap closed to four Merit within an hour. It was all right. We'd had the morning.

---

We slept on the floor of the hall that night in a ring, against the base of a column, taking watches. The scale creaked above us every so often, a long low sound like a ship.

I had the second watch. Nora had the third, and she came and sat beside me an hour early, wrapped in the hotel blanket she'd stopped bothering to return.

"Vesper said someone was hired," she said quietly. "For Day Thirty."

"Yes."

"And that he'd do it the same way you died." She pulled the blanket tighter. "You never told me how you died. Not properly. You said the hundredth floor and Damon and a door."

So I told her properly. The altar. *Eli, I need your eyes on this.* Forty people backing out through the far door while I turned around to say the reading had come back strange. *You were always the cheapest thing in the room.* The ceiling.

She listened all the way through without interrupting, which she almost never did.

"So the method is, he puts somebody in front of the thing," she said, "and walks out the back."

"That's the method."

"It's always him, isn't it. The hired someone."

"Probably." I looked down the hall, at the windows, at the pale hands lying still on their counters. "Vesper doesn't waste words. If she'd meant somebody new, she'd have said a name. She wanted me to figure it out, so it wouldn't count as her telling me."

"Then why does it feel worse that we know?"

"Because now we have to watch for it," I said. "Knowing is the expensive part. Not knowing is free right up until the bill comes."

Nora was quiet for a while. Above us the beam groaned and settled.

"He can't make me hold anything," she said at last. "I'm not in his guild. You voided me."

"I know."

"So he'd have to make it so I had no choice." She said it slowly, testing the shape of the words. "So that holding was the only move left."

I didn't answer that. I didn't have an answer yet. I wrote it in the ledger instead, under the day's numbers, in small letters: *no choice = his method.* Then I gave her the watch and lay down and didn't sleep.

On the morning of Day Nineteen, at nine o'clock, a window opened in front of me in the Exchange's gold.

**[Transfer Received: 180 Merit]**
**[From: Damon Kreel]**
**[Memo: Interest, Week 2. Vanguard Charter Credit.]**
**[Source of Funds: Ashgrave Recoveries — Field Agent Stipend.]**

**[Balance: 1,575 Merit]**

Nora read it over my shoulder. She'd stopped asking to. She just did it.

"Ashgrave," she said. "He's paying you with their money. Money they pay him for telling them where I sleep."

"Yes."

"That's gross."

"Money doesn't smell," I said. "Where it came from does." I wrote it in the ledger. *Day 19: Damon interest 180 (Ashgrave source).* "One thousand five hundred seventy-five. Nineteen twenty-five short for the twenty-third, before the House."

"Four days."

"Four days and three hours."

She looked at the line in the ledger, and then at me, and then she took the pen and wrote, under Damon's line, *Hold the Line: 0 uses*, and pressed so hard that the paper tore a little at the end of the *s*.

---

The vault door was at the far end of the hall, under the great scale's empty right-hand pan. We'd found it by midafternoon on Day Nineteen: a round bronze door three times Bram's height, with a dial in the center that turned on its own, slowly, and a slot below it, like the night-deposit slot at a bank.

**[The Teller's Vault]**
**[Entry is final.]**

A party from Zone Two had made camp by the forty-fourth column with every intention of going in at dawn. They had the kid Appraiser. They had numbers. The first clear on this floor was four thousand Merit to the region, and a quarter of that for whoever had contributed most, and I had nineteen hundred and twenty-five reasons not to wait for dawn.

At eight minutes to midnight on Day Nineteen, we went in.

The vault was round, and very tall, and empty except for a counter at the far side. Behind the counter, a grille. Behind the grille, darkness. The floor was a single polished disc of marble, and a seam of brass ran straight across it from one wall to the other, through the middle, under our feet.

I was looking at the seam when I heard the door behind us grind.

Not closing. Opening.

They came in fast and in good order, the way they'd been drilled since Day Zero. Gray armbands. Polished gear. Fifteen of them, with Damon Kreel at the front, in his armor, wearing a cord of Ashgrave gray tied neatly around his left wrist like a watchband.

**[Damon Kreel — Warrior (Lv. 15)]**
**[Affiliation: Vanguard (Guild Master); Ashgrave Recoveries (Field Agent).]**

Behind them, the round door rolled shut with a sound like a bank closing for the weekend.

**[Entry is final.]**

"Eli," said Damon pleasantly. He looked around the vault, at the counter, at the seam, at Nora standing beside Bram with her axe low. He looked, last of all, at me, and smiled, and it was exactly the smile from the hundredth floor.

"Let's clear it together," he said. "I want to show you what I found out."

Under our feet, with a long grinding shriek of brass on marble, the floor split in half.
