# Chapter 4: Cost of Bait

The water came up in the night, and I was awake for it.

It didn't rush. There was no roar, no wave crashing through the lobby doors. At exactly the time the notice had promised, the surface of the black lake outside simply lifted, smooth as a sheet being pulled up over a bed, and the ground floor of Harlan Hall went under. I watched it from the window. The mailboxes. The front desk. The flyer for Thursday night trivia.

**[The water has risen. Current level: Ground Floor.]**
**[Time until next rise: 11:59:59]**
**[First Installment: 692 Merit. Due: 59:48:02]**
**[Balance: 0 Merit]**

Nora was still asleep against my shoulder. I didn't wake her. I sat there and did arithmetic until the green sky got a little paler, which was the closest Zone Nine came to morning.

Sixty hours. Six hundred and ninety-two Merit. Four more rises before the roof went under.

And a library across a lake with a monster in it.

---

"Happy birthday, Mom," Nora said when she woke up. She said it to the window, very quietly, and then she stood up and stretched and turned around, and she was Nora again. "Okay. What's the plan?"

The others were waking up too. Eleven students in a study lounge with a vending machine against the door. I'd read the RA's clipboard twice in the night and knew all their classes by heart. The RA's name was Owen. He was twenty-one, a Cleric, and he'd slept sitting up with the clipboard on his knees like he was afraid someone would steal it.

"The plan," I said, "is that we make money."

Owen blinked at me. "Money."

"Merit." I pulled up the zone rules and turned the window so the room could see it. You couldn't show someone your status, but anyone could read a notice if you held it out to them. "The water goes up one floor every twelve hours. The only way it stops is if something called the Drowned Librarian dies. The Librarian is in there." I pointed at the cold light across the lake. "To get there, we need a way across the water, a way past what lives in the water, and enough levels that we don't die the second we walk in. All of that costs Merit. So first, we make Merit."

"How?" said one of the Warriors, a big quiet guy with a rugby shirt.

"The same way everyone does." I nodded at the door, and the scratches on the other side of it. "We kill things."

---

The things were Silt Leeches.

They came up the stairwells every hour or so, the way I'd seen them come up in a hundred stories people told about Zone Nine afterward. Gray, slick, the length of a forearm, with a round mouth full of little hooks. Alone, one was a nuisance. In a group of six or seven, they could strip a grown man to the bone in about the time it took him to scream.

"They go for whoever's closest," I told Nora. We were standing in the hallway outside the lounge, and I could hear them on the stairs, a wet slapping sound like someone dropping raw steak down a staircase. "Unless something gives them a better reason."

"And I'm the better reason."

"Hold the Line makes you the most interesting thing in the room. They'll all go for you. You don't need to kill them. You just need to not get bitten."

"Oh, is *that* all?"

"You've got a fire axe. Use the flat. Keep them on the floor. Owen and the other two Clerics stay behind you. Everyone else stands at the sides and hits whatever you knock down." I looked at her. "You don't have to do this. I can figure out something else."

She gave me a look I remembered from when she was nine and I'd told her she was too young to ride the big roller coaster. Then she hefted the axe.

"I picked the shield class," she said. "Let me use the shield class."

The first leech came around the corner of the stairwell, and my sister planted her feet, and said, loudly and without much conviction, "Hey. Over here."

**[Nora Marsh used Hold the Line.]**

Every leech in the stairwell turned toward her at once.

---

It went badly for the first ten minutes and then it went well.

The first ten minutes, Nora got bitten twice, once on the calf and once on the forearm, and one of the Warriors swung at a leech on the floor and hit the wall so hard he dropped his sword. Owen healed her with shaking hands. I stood at the back and appraised everything that moved, because that was my job, and because I'd found something useful in the numbers.

**[Silt Leech — Tutorial Beast (Lv. 3)]**
**[Market Value of Parts: Hide 1 Merit | Hooks 2 Merit | Core (Tail) 6 Merit]**

"The tail!" I shouted. "Their heads are armored, don't bother! Hit them just in front of the tail!"

After that, it went well.

Nora learned fast. She'd always learned physical things fast. She stopped swinging the axe and started using the flat of the blade to sweep the leeches off her legs and pin them to the floor, and every time one hit the carpet, a Warrior stepped in and stabbed it just in front of the tail. By the third wave, they had a rhythm. By the fifth, the big quiet Warrior in the rugby shirt was grinning.

I kept count. Every core went into a pillowcase I'd taken from one of the dorm rooms.

By noon, we had twenty-two cores, and Nora was level five.

**[Level Up! Appraiser Lv. 3]**

I'd gotten two levels just from being in the party and appraising. It was more than I'd earned in my entire first week of the old life. Back then, I'd been hiding.

---

Here's something about Day One that nobody talks about. The way most groups split loot in the tutorials was: whoever killed it, kept it. That was fair on paper. In practice, it meant the Warriors got rich and the Clerics got nothing, and by Day Two, groups that had worked well together were stabbing each other in the back over a few Merit.

I'd watched Damon run it that way for seven years, and I'd kept the books for him while he did it.

So after the fifth wave, I dumped the pillowcase out on the lounge table in front of everyone and appraised every core out loud.

"Six Merit each," I said. "Twenty-two cores. A hundred and thirty-two, total. That's the real price. If anybody tries to buy one from you after the tutorial for less than six, they're robbing you."

Everybody looked at me. Owen, very slowly, wrote *132* on his clipboard.

"We split it evenly," I said. "Everyone who stood in that hallway gets the same share. Clerics too. Nora gets double, because she's the one getting bitten."

"What do you get?" said the rugby shirt.

"A normal share," I said. "And I want to borrow the whole pile tonight. I'll give it back, plus some. I need it for the crossing."

There was a short silence. I'd expected that. I was a stranger who'd appeared out of a flooded basement, and I was asking them to hand me their money.

"He's good for it," Nora said, from the couch, with her bandaged leg up on a cushion. Then she thought about it. "Mostly."

That got a laugh. Owen looked at me, then at the clipboard, then wrote a line under the number. I leaned over later and read it. *Loaned to Eli (Nora's brother). Due back after crossing.*

It was, I thought, the first honest contract anyone had offered me in seven years.

---

The crossing was the problem.

I'd spent all morning at the window watching the Hollow Matron. You could see it, if you knew what to look for. A long pale ridge breaking the surface of the lake, slow and regular, circling. It never went far from one spot: the flooded entrance to Harlan Hall's own basement, just below us.

That was where the compass had gone. It had taken the most valuable thing within thirty meters back to its nest, and now it was sitting on it.

**[Appraise]**

**[Hollow Matron — Zone Beast (Lv. 19)]**
**[Behavior: Drawn to the object of highest value within 30 meters.]**
**[Currently guarding: Gilded Compass (True Value: 220 Merit).]**

"Okay," Nora said, when I explained it. "So it's a giant fish that likes shiny things."

"It likes *valuable* things. It's not the same. The compass barely shines."

"How does it know what things are worth?"

"The same way I do," I said. "It appraises."

That thought had kept me up half the night. Everything in this world had a price. The Exchange had built itself on it. It made sense that some of its monsters could see prices, too. I'd just never met one that went hunting by them.

"So," Nora said slowly, "if we want to cross, we need something worth more than two hundred and twenty Merit. And we need to throw it somewhere the fish isn't."

"Yes."

"Do we have anything worth two hundred and twenty Merit?"

"We have a hundred and thirty-two Merit's worth of cores in a pillowcase," I said. "And I think I know how to make it worth more."

---

It took the whole afternoon and three more waves of leeches.

The Exchange had a funny way of counting value. A single leech core was worth six Merit. Twenty-two loose cores were worth six Merit each, twenty-two times over. But the Matron wasn't hunting *Merit.* It was hunting *objects,* and it picked the single most valuable one in range. Twenty-two cores rattling around in a lake were twenty-two tiny objects, and none of them beat a compass.

So I tied them up.

**[Pillowcase (Contents: 22 Silt Cores) — True Value: 132 Merit]**

The system saw it as one object. I'd been right about that. But it was still less than the compass.

We went back to the stairwell. By the end of the third wave, the pillowcase had thirty-eight cores in it, and I had two more levels. I tied the top shut with a phone charger cable and appraised it again.

**[Pillowcase (Contents: 38 Silt Cores) — True Value: 228 Merit]**

Two hundred and twenty-eight. Eight more than the compass.

"We're going to throw away all of our money," Nora said, looking at it. "To distract a fish."

"It's called an investment."

"It's called *throwing money in a lake,* Eli."

"If we don't cross, the water keeps coming up and none of the money matters." I held the pillowcase in both hands. It was heavier than it looked, and it wasn't mine, and I was about to throw it into the dark. "It's the same math as the transfer. You spend what you've got to stay in the game."

She looked at me for a long moment.

"You said *the transfer*," she said. "Like that's a normal thing. What transfer? How much did it cost you to get here?"

"Later."

"You keep saying later."

"I know."

---

We went at dusk, if you could call it that. The green sky got darker, the library's light got brighter, and the water outside the window rose a little higher up the brick wall, getting ready for its next climb.

**[Time until next rise: 01:12:40]**

The way across was a set of four aluminum rowboats from the campus boathouse. Nora and the rugby shirt had seen them that morning, from the roof: all four of them bumping against the boathouse's upper windows, tied to the dock that was now three meters under water. Two of the Rangers had gone out along the roofs of the old walkway between buildings and cut them free with a kitchen knife. It had taken them three hours and one very close call with a leech swarm.

We got into them from the second-floor window of the dorm next door. Nora, me, Owen, the rugby shirt, and two others in the first boat. The rest would wait with the second group of boats until we signaled.

I held the pillowcase on my lap.

"Where do you throw it?" Owen whispered.

"East," I said. "The old science building. It's farther than thirty meters from the library, and farther than thirty meters from the nest. When the Matron goes for it, it'll leave the compass behind, and we'll have a clear line to the library for about as long as it takes to figure out it can't eat cores through a pillowcase."

"How long is that?"

"I don't know."

"You don't *know?*"

"I've never done this before," I said, which was entirely true.

The rugby shirt was the strongest of us. He stood up in the rocking boat, took the pillowcase, and threw it with a grunt, a high overhand arc that carried out over the black water toward the dark bulk of the science building. It hit the lake with a heavy splash.

For three seconds, nothing happened.

Then the surface near the dorm's flooded entrance bulged, and split, and a ridge of pale fin as long as a bus slid out of the water and turned east. The whole boat rocked in its wake. Something enormous moved under us, so close that I could feel the water pull at the aluminum hull, and then it was gone, a long V of ripples heading for the science building.

"*Row*," I said.

---

We rowed.

I don't remember much of it. The oars. The cold. Owen muttering a prayer that I don't think was addressed to anything the Exchange sold. The library getting bigger, its arched windows glowing like the inside of a refrigerator. Somewhere behind us, far off to the east, a loud wet crash, and then another, and then a sound like an animal throwing a tantrum.

The boat hit the library's front steps with a hollow bang. The water here came up to the top of the third step. Above that, the stone was dry. There was a wide landing, a row of pillars, and a pair of tall wooden doors, shut.

We tied up. I signaled back to the dorm with a flashlight. Three flashes. *Clear. Come now.*

Then I walked up the steps to the doors.

**[Location: Hale Memorial Library]**
**[Zone Boss: The Drowned Librarian]**
**[Warning: Entry is final until the Boss is slain or all party members are removed.]**

"Removed," Owen said, reading over my shoulder. "What does *removed* mean?"

"It means dead," said Nora.

I wasn't listening. I was looking at the door.

Someone had carved into it. Not in the wood itself, but in the pale waterline stain that ran across both doors at about chest height, as if the flood had come up this high once, a long time ago, and gone down again. The letters were scratched into the stain with something sharp. A knife, maybe. They were uneven and cramped, and they leaned hard to the right, and the lowercase *t*s were crossed too high, like little lowercase *f*s.

I knew that handwriting. I'd been writing with it for thirty-two years, if you counted the ones that nobody else remembered.

*DON'T LET HER HOLD THE LINE.*
*— E.*
