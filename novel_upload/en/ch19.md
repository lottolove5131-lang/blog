# Chapter 19: Line Holder

The floor didn't break. It unhinged.

The vault was round, a drum of white marble forty meters across, and a seam of bronze ran through the middle of it from wall to wall, straight as a ruled line. It had been there the whole time. I'd walked over it. I'd appraised the floor when the door sealed behind us and it had told me *Floor: marble, decorative*, and I'd believed it, because floors don't usually lie.

This one was a scale pan.

The far half of the vault, the half with the Teller's window at the end of it, dropped a hand's width with a sound like a safe door settling. Then it stopped, and tilted, and began to sink, slow and level, the way the pan of a market scale goes down under a bag of rice.

Nora was on it.

We'd come in the way we'd come into every room for a week: two shields, one axe. Bram on the left, Owen on the right, Nora between them with the axe held low. The seam had run exactly between Bram's boots and hers. A hand's width at first. When I looked again, it was wider than a stride.

"Nora!"

Bram moved before I did. Two and a half meters of Bulwark turned, took three running steps, and jumped, and for a second I thought he'd make it. Then the far pan dropped another meter and his boots hit the lip of nothing, and Jonah and two of Damon's people caught him by the straps and hauled him back before he went over.

"Off me," Bram said. "Off me, I can still—"

"You'd land on her," Jonah said. "Look."

The far pan was four meters down now, then six, then eight. Nora stood in the middle of it with her axe low and her face tipped toward us. She didn't shout. She was looking for the thing that had happened, the way she'd looked for the water in Zone Nine.

There was a line in her half of our ledger, in her handwriting, that she'd started the night I made the rule. *Hold the Line: 0.* Every night she'd carried it forward. Through the end of Day Nineteen, it still said zero.

I looked too.

Damon's people had been carrying packs when they came in behind us. Big ones, canvas, the kind porters use. I'd noticed and filed it under *Vanguard is broke and carries its own furniture.* The packs were on the far pan now. Six of them, thrown in a loose ring around the place Nora had been standing, spilling armor plates and ingot bars across the marble.

Weight. They'd loaded her side.

**[Appraise]**

**[Vanguard Guild Equipment (Shared), 6 packs: Market Value 410 Merit | True Value 410 Merit]**
**[Weight: 388 kg.]**

Three hundred and eighty-eight kilos of guild property, bought on a charter loan I owned, sitting in a ring around my sister.

---

The far pan settled at the bottom with a soft chime.

From up here the vault was two floors now. Ours, level with the door we'd come through. Hers, eight meters below, a marble pit with walls of polished stone. Between them, at the point where a scale's beam would sit, stood the counter.

It was a tower of brass. I'd taken it for a pillar. It ran from the bottom of the pit to the ceiling, and at the bottom, facing Nora, there was a grille window with a ledge and a bell. At the top, on our level, facing us, there was a drawer. A long brass drawer with a keyhole the size of my fist, and three heaps of loose gold coins around it that were already beginning to stand up.

**[Gold-Coin Golem, Lv. 14 — x3]**
**[Weakness: the face-coin. Strike it and the body forgets its shape.]**

And behind the grille, at the bottom of the pit, something leaned forward.

**[The Teller, Lv. 19 (Floor Boss)]**

It was tall and narrow, stacked out of coin rolls like a column of quarters someone had wrapped in brass paper. Sleeve garters. A green visor of hammered gold. Hands like the brass scoops grocers use for flour. It had no face under the visor, only a slot, and the slot spoke in a voice like a receipt being torn off.

"Next."

Nora took one step back. "Eli."

"I see it."

"What is it?"

"A teller," I said. "Don't touch the bell."

The room's gold text came up over the grille before I could appraise anything.

**[ENTRY REGISTERED: VANGUARD (Guild Account), represented by Damon Kreel.]**
**[Accompanying: Eli Marsh +4.]**
**[Entry is final.]**

Accompanying. He'd put us on his account.

---

Damon was standing at the lip of the pit with his hands behind his back. Warrior's armor, the good set, not the borrowed one from the courthouse. The gray cord on his left wrist. His smile was exactly where he'd left it.

"I told you," he said. "We clear it together."

"You loaded her pan."

"I balanced the room." He nodded down at the pit. "Ashgrave keeps files on every floor it expects to collect on. The Counting House is a scale. This vault splits on the first heavy step past the seam, and the heavier pan goes to the window. The window bills. That's all." He shrugged. "I read the fine print, Eli. You taught me that."

Below us the bell rang by itself. Once.

**[THE TELLER: Settlement due.]**
**[Entry Fee: 400 Merit per entrant. Billing cycle: 30 seconds.]**
**[Unpaid amounts will be collected in kind.]**

"In kind," Owen said beside me. His riot shield was on one arm and his clipboard was in the other hand, and he didn't seem to know which one to put down. "What does in kind mean?"

The grille answered him. Brass chains came out of it, thin as watch chains, a dozen of them, and went up the walls of the pit like vines and over the lip and across our floor. One touched Owen's ankle. He went down on one knee and a gray line of his health bar simply went away, as cleanly as if someone had drawn a line through it.

**[Collected in kind: Owen Park — 400 Merit equivalent.]**

Another chain found a Vanguard Ranger behind Damon. She screamed. Another went for Jonah and he cut it with his short sword and it re-formed like water. One wrapped around my wrist, cold, and I felt it take something out of me, about as much as a hard fall down a flight of stairs.

The chains drew back into the grille.

Thirty seconds.

---

"Talk," Bram said to me. He was crouched behind his shield at the lip of the pit, looking down. "You're the one who talks. Talk fast."

So I did the math out loud, because that's what I do.

"Four hundred a head every thirty seconds. I have one thousand five hundred seventy-five." I'd counted it this morning, the way I counted it every morning now, with Nora reading over my shoulder. Seven ninety-five after the second installment, six hundred from the windows, one eighty of Damon's interest. The Clipboard House money sat in Owen's column, two hundred of it, and I counted that too, because I always count everything. "Seventeen seventy-five with the House. That pays four entry fees. Once. There are five of us. Then the next bill comes in thirty seconds."

"Then don't pay," Jonah said.

"Then it collects in kind. From all of us. Every thirty seconds." I looked at the drawer. "Owen took one hit and lost a quarter of his health. Four hits, he's on the floor."

"The drawer," Owen said.

"The drawer."

**[Appraise]**

**[The Teller — Market Value: 2,600 Merit | True Value: 5,100 Merit]**
**[Bills are drawn from the Till (upper level). Breaking the Till ends issuance. Till integrity: 100%.]**
**[Till guarded by Gold-Coin Golems (reform from loose coin, 20s).]**
**[Note: Billing follows the party. A designated Line Holder is billed as sole debtor for the party.]**

I read the last line twice.

Then I looked down into the pit, and Nora was already looking up at me, because Nora had been reading my face since she was three.

---

The bell rang again.

The chains came out, more of them this time, and this time I watched where they went. They went to everyone. Bram took two on the shield and they wrapped the rim and took their four hundred anyway, through the steel, through his arm. A Vanguard man near the door fell and didn't get up for a long, ugly moment. Damon stepped out of the way of one with no hurry at all, and it went past him and found the man behind him.

None of them went to Nora. She was below the chains. They went up past her, over her head, the way a waiter goes past a table that hasn't ordered.

"Why isn't it hitting her?" Jonah said.

"Because she hasn't stepped up to the window," Damon said. "Yet."

I turned and looked at him.

"She'll hold," he said. Not unkindly. The way he used to tell me a sword was worth less than I'd said. "She always holds. It's in the file, Eli. Zone Nine. The library. A boss on the first floor, she was going to do it there too, I hear, until you shouted at her. When her people are getting hit, she steps forward. Every time. That's what a Warden is."

"And when she steps forward," I said, "the window bills her alone."

"And the rest of us walk out." He spread his hands. "Nobody else gets a scratch. It's a very good deal."

"Ashgrave's deal."

"Ashgrave's file," he said. "My deal."

I understood the rest of it then, the part he didn't say. A Line Holder takes the whole party's bill. Her health goes down. Her armor gets marked. Somewhere in a gray ledger, a line gets written: *Collateral impaired, Day Twenty, Floor Two, in the course of a voluntary line-hold.* I didn't know yet what that line was for. I knew the Ashgrave file didn't contain anything that wasn't for something.

There was a note in my own ledger, two pages back, in my handwriting from the day before. *no choice = his method.* I'd written it about the hundredth floor. I hadn't thought I'd need it again so soon.

"You were a nineteen-day-old guild master," I said. "Now you're a nineteen-day-old errand boy."

Damon glanced at the clock in the corner of his vision. "Twenty," he said. "It turned midnight a minute ago. It's Day Twenty."

---

We went for the drawer.

It was the only thing I could think of, so it was what we did. Bram took the front and walked into the nearest golem shield-first, and it came apart against him into a hissing wave of coins and stood up again two meters to his left. Jonah put an arrow through the second one's face-coin and it slumped into a heap, and twenty seconds later the heap stood up again with a new face. Owen stayed on Bram. I went for the keyhole with my dagger, which was the stupidest thing I did that day, and the third golem put the back of its hand into my chest and sat me down on the marble ten feet away.

**[Till integrity: 97%.]**

Three percent.

The bell rang.

I took a chain on the shoulder and felt four hundred Merit of me go away. Jonah took one. Owen took one and swore, which I'd never heard him do. Behind us, Damon's people were backed against the door in a tight knot, taking their bills and not moving, because Damon had told them not to move. Damon's people, bleeding four hundred at a time, waiting for my sister.

I sat on the marble and did the second calculation.

The golems re-formed in twenty seconds. Break one, and before you'd finished breaking the next, the first was standing up out of its own coins. We had four people on the drawer, and one of them was a Cleric who couldn't stop healing, and one of them was me. I'd taken the drawer from a hundred to ninety-seven in about forty seconds of everything we had. At that rate, with nobody getting billed, it was a little under twenty-two minutes.

We weren't going to have twenty-two minutes. We were going to have about two, and then Owen would go down, and then Jonah, and then me.

Unless someone stood at the window.

**[Appraise]** — I pointed it down, into the pit.

**[Nora Marsh — Warden — Lv. 14]**
**[If Hold the Line is used: billed as sole debtor. Estimated endurance under full party billing: 04:10.]**

Four minutes and ten seconds of my sister alone at a window, so that four people could hit a drawer without being billed.

Four minutes wasn't twenty-two. But with nobody getting billed, Owen wouldn't be healing, he'd be swinging. Bram wouldn't be bracing, he'd be pushing. Damon's fifteen might even move. I didn't know how fast fifteen extra people broke a drawer. I didn't know if they'd lift a hand. I didn't know anything except the two numbers, and the two numbers were telling me something I'd sworn in a burned corridor and a flooded library and a dead man's handwriting that I would never let happen.

*Don't let her hold the line.*

She'd held it for ten minutes on the eighty-eighth floor. In another life. With a man who looked like me screaming at her from the back.

---

"Eli!"

Her voice came up out of the pit. Not scared. Angry, the way she was angry at the dorm door on Day Zero.

"I can see the chains," she called. "Owen's down to half. You're bleeding. What's the number?"

"Don't."

"What's the *number*, Eli?"

I didn't answer. I stood up. My chest hurt where the golem had hit it, and my shoulder hurt where the chain had been, and the drawer said ninety-six percent.

"No," I called down. "We break the drawer. You stay out of the window. You stay at the back of that pit and you don't touch the bell, you hear me? That's the rule. That's the one rule."

"That's not a plan. That's a rule."

"It's both."

"Owen's at forty percent," Nora said, very evenly. She could see his health bar from down there. She'd always been able to read a room. "Jonah's at sixty. You're at fifty-something. Bram's taking two a cycle on that shield. The Vanguard woman by the door isn't getting up." A pause. "I did the math too, Eli. I'm not stupid. I've been reading your ledger for a week."

The bell rang.

The chains came.

One wrapped Owen's wrist, the one with the clipboard, and he made a sound I'd never heard from him and the clipboard slid out of his hand and across the marble. Jonah went to one knee. I took mine on the ribs, and for a moment there was nothing in the world but the floor.

When I could see again, Damon was looking at the pit with his head tilted, the way you watch a price on the board when you're sure it's about to move.

"Ten seconds," he said quietly, to nobody. "She lasts about ten seconds, the file says. After the second friendly drops."

Owen wasn't getting up.

I crawled to him. His health bar was a thin red line. I put my hand on his shoulder like it would do something, and I looked over the lip of the pit.

Nora had walked to the window.

She was standing in front of the grille with her axe hanging loose at her side, and she was looking up at me. Her face was very calm. It was the face from the library roof, the one that had decided something and didn't intend to argue about it.

"Nora. *Nora.* Don't you dare—"

"Eli," she said. Not *idiot*, not *stupid*. Just my name, quiet, like she was apologizing for something she'd already bought. "I'm sorry."

She reached up and rang the bell herself.

**[Nora Marsh used Hold the Line.]**
